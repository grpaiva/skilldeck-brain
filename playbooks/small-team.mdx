## When to use this

A manager who fits the [ICP](/company/icp-and-market) has met with us and is interested in Skill Deck.

## Prerequisites

- The manager's company fits the ICP: 50 to 500 employees, hybrid or remote.
- The manager is buying for their own team.
- The people on the plan spend much of their week in meetings, so evidence accumulates.

## Why a month

Confidence in a Skill score grows with the number of evaluations behind it, and every meeting adds evaluations. The more meetings a person has, the faster their confidence climbs. Someone with a heavy week of meetings can reach high confidence within that week. A month gives everyone on the plan, including people with fewer meetings, enough evidence for a profile worth reading.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Show the manager their own meeting analysis">
    If the manager is interested, they create a Skill Deck account, and we share the meeting we just had with them. Skill Deck evaluates it for them, so they get a small, real sample of how the product works.

    This needs our meeting bot in the call. Share within 7 days of the meeting, because the share expires when the transcript leaves the cache. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data#sharing).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Offer the Small Team plan">
    One month, five people on the manager's team, no long-term contract. The price is on [Canonical facts](/overview/canonical-facts#pricing). It is the clearest test of whether the manager is willing to pay for the tool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a 30-minute onboarding">
    Show how Skill Deck works, how it connects to the calendar, and what the team view looks like, then leave time for questions. In the same session:

    - Make sure all five people connect a calendar on day one. If the team meets on Zoom, they also need the Zoom connection.
    - Say what a manager can and cannot see, from [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data).
    - Explain that the more meetings someone has, the faster their confidence climbs. A person with a heavy meeting week can reach high confidence within that week.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hand over the Manager Playbook">
    Give the manager the monthly 15-minute feedback conversation from the [Manager Playbook](/playbooks/manager-coaching/overview), and have them book it with each person on the plan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check in mid-month">
    Around the two-week mark, confirm that calendars are still connected and evidence is landing. Fix integration problems now, while the second half of the month can still produce something.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close with the manager">
    At the end of the month, go through the results with the manager and find out whether they want to expand to more people or to other teams. Convert them to the plan that matches the team's size, with no discount.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Definition of done

- All five people connected a calendar in week one.
- Each of them has enough evidence for a meaningful profile by the end of the month.
- The manager has seen the team view and at least one person in detail.
- A plan for the full team is agreed, or you know exactly why it was not.

## Failure modes

**Waiting too long to share the meeting analysis.** After 7 days the transcript is gone, and the sample goes with it.

**Calendars never get connected.** This is the most common way a month produces nothing. Treat the connection rate as the plan's health metric from day one.

**Too few meetings.** Confidence grows with the number of meetings Skill Deck evaluates. A person with many meetings can reach high confidence in their first week. A person with few meetings stays at low confidence until more meetings come in. Put people on the plan who spend much of their week in meetings.

**Skipping the privacy conversation.** It does not go away. It goes underground.
