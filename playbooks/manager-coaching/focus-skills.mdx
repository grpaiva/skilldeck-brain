Focus Skills are the skills you pick for each person, and we recommend 3 to 5. Skill Deck keeps assessing all 58 and goes deeper on these. The Journal covers everything it saw, and gives the Focus Skills their own feedback section after every meeting.

## Where to set them

On the team page. Set them for the whole team at once when everyone shares a priority, or one person at a time, which is what you'll do once people are in different places.

## Who picks them

The leader. Picking them together with the person is fine, and often a good conversation, but the call is yours. You're the one who knows what the role needs next.

The person can see their own Focus Skills. They can't change them without your permission. They'll see your choice from day one, so tell them why you picked those and not others.

## Wait a week before you pick

Don't set Focus Skills on day one. Give the person at least a week of normal use, so there's a Deck worth reading and a Journal with real meetings in it.

## How to pick

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with the role">
    What does this person have to be good at to do the job well? A new people manager needs delegation. An account lead needs discovery. Start here, because this is the skill that matters whether or not it stands out in the data.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then the Deck">
    Where does it sit below what the role needs?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then the Journal">
    Once a few meetings have been assessed, which Growth Opportunity keeps coming back on its own?
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keeping or changing Focus Skills

This is the second thing you settle in the last two minutes of the [monthly conversation](/playbooks/manager-coaching/monthly). Expect to keep them most months.

Keep when the skill still matters for the role and the person is still moving on it. Slow movement still counts, and a skill that changes every month never gathers enough evidence to be worth reading.

Change when one of these is true:

- The skill is now a consistent strength, with the evidence to back it up.
- The role changed, so the skill no longer matters most.
- Four weeks produced almost no evidence, because their meetings don't create the situation. Change the skill, or change which meetings Skill Deck sees.

<Warning>
  Don't change after one bad meeting, or because another skill looks more interesting. Changing too often is the most common way this stops producing anything useful. Two or three months on a skill is a reasonable floor.
</Warning>
