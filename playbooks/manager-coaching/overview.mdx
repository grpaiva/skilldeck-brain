Three management levels — Weekly, Monthly, and Quarterly — so a manager can match their coaching cadence to team size, seniority, and bandwidth without inventing a process from scratch.

## What the manager owns

Skill Deck maps and develops people on autopilot. The manager's job is not to replace that autopilot. It is to do three things.

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="Coach" icon="messages-square">
    Turn signals into clearer practice and better judgment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Guide" icon="compass">
    Keep the right Skill and Goal in focus.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hold accountable" icon="check-circle">
    Make progress — and evidence volume — a regular conversation.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## The core rule

<Warning>
  **Tracks need about two weeks minimum. Do not churn Skills.**

  This is the rule the whole playbook protects. A manager who switches a member's Skill every week guarantees that no Track ever reaches completion, and the product never gets to show what it does.
</Warning>

## Read this first

Levels are named by **how often you meet**, not by how often Skill Deck messages the member. The product still sends Monday Prep and Friday Review every week for active Tracks. What changes is the manager's coaching cadence.

## The whole playbook in six moves

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a level">
    Weekly, Monthly, or Quarterly for your team, and put the matching calendar holds in place. Align Quarterly to your Cycle or quarter dates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up the team page">
    Enable Skill Tracks, and set automation and the whitelist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cover everyone">
    Every member has an Active Track — or a clear unlocking or pending plan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prep the same way every time">
    Before each coaching meeting: **Overview → Journal → Deck** on the member page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close every meeting">
    A Goal decision (default: stay the course), one or two practice behaviors, and an evidence plan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Protect the Track">
    Tracks need about two weeks minimum to complete and can run longer. Do not churn Skills.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose your management level

Skill Deck adapts to how you already lead. Two questions decide the level: **what is your level of involvement**, and **how often do you usually talk to your team about their development?**

You can switch levels as your needs change.

| Level | Your meeting cadence | What you typically cover | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Weekly** | ~30 min every week | One active Track in motion — practice quality, evidence volume, Prep/Review themes. Expect several meetings on the same Track. | Critical Skills, junior members, new to Skill Deck, small teams |
| **Monthly** | ~45–60 min once a month | Progress across two to four weeks of Track work — often one Track still active, sometimes a recent Wrap-Up plus the next Skill. About 3 meetings per Cycle. | Steady teams, mixed seniority, limited manager bandwidth |
| **Quarterly** | ~45–60 min once per Cycle | The full Cycle story — Tracks completed, switched, or rolled; Deck movement; next-Cycle focus. Async skim of Wrap-Ups only if needed. | Senior or self-directed teams, large spans of control |

### How to choose

<Steps>
  <Step title="Team size">
    Weekly is hard above roughly six to eight direct reports, unless meetings stay short and async prep is strong.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seniority">
    More senior members usually fit Monthly or Quarterly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Skill criticality">
    Role-defining Skills — Delegation for a new people manager, for example — push toward Weekly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence maturity">
    If the team is still unlocking Skills or connecting calendars, start Weekly until the autopilot has signal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You can mix">
    Weekly for two people on a stretch Skill, Monthly for everyone else.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Protect Track duration">
    Whichever level you pick, avoid switching Skills so often that Tracks never reach the two-week completion window.
  </Step>
</Steps>
