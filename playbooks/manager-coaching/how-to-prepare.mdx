To prepare for the monthly conversation in Skill Deck, you have three things to look at: the Deck, the Journal and the Monthly Report. Bring questions and leave the conclusions for the conversation.

<Note>
  The Monthly Report is described here as the playbook PDF describes it. It is not live in the product yet.
</Note>

## How the analysis works

Every meeting is assessed across 58 skills. That gives dozens of assessments per meeting, each tied to a specific moment where the skill appeared.

The same person can be strong on a skill at minute 8 and weak on it at minute 34, and Skill Deck records both. Nobody uses a skill the same way all the time. How it comes out depends on the moment and who's in the room.

So there's far more information than a fifteen-minute conversation can hold. The Journal picks out what matters in each meeting, and the Monthly Report adds the month up for you.

## Step 1. Read the Deck

Four things you can notice in the Deck:

1. **Top strengths.** What is this person reliably good at, according to the evidence?
2. **Outliers.** Anything unusually high or unusually low against the rest of their profile. That's where the interesting conversations are.
3. **The gap.** Where the Deck sits below what the role needs. You'll come back to this one across months.
4. **Confidence.** Check it before you trust the other three. A high score at low confidence means the skill hasn't been seen in enough meetings yet, so treat it as provisional.

You're collecting the broad picture here. Step 2 is where it gets concrete, against the month's meetings.

## Step 2. Read the Journal and the Monthly Report

The Journal holds every meeting from the month. Each assessed meeting gives you five findings.

| Finding | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Focus Skill feedback** | How the person used the skill you selected, in this specific meeting |
| **Top Strength** | The skill that held up across many moments of the meeting, with good evaluation throughout. It rewards consistency over a single peak |
| **Growth Opportunity** | The clearest chance the person had to do better in this meeting |
| **Best Moment** | The single highest-rated moment. A peak, which can appear in an area that is otherwise shaky |
| **Most Demonstrated Skill** | Whatever appeared most often, five, six, seven, eight times, regardless of score. It tells you what the meeting demanded of the person |

You don't have to pick meetings. The Monthly Report compiles these findings across everything that happened in the month: the skills the person demonstrated most, their growth opportunities, their top strengths and their best moments.

Skim the Journal for the meetings you want to remember, then build your questions from the Report.

## Step 3. Listen to one Advisor Pick

The Advisor Pick sits apart from the meeting analysis. It's a short curated audio that teaches one technique the person can put to use.

Pick one from the Journal and listen to it all the way through. You need to know what it taught before you can ask whether it landed.
