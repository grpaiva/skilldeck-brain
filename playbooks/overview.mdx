The Company and Product sections describe what is true. Playbooks describe what to do.

A playbook belongs here when all three are true:

1. We do it more than once.
2. Doing it inconsistently costs us something.
3. Someone new — or an agent — could execute it from the page alone.

Anything that fails those tests is a note, not a playbook. Keep it out.

## Playbook format

Keep every playbook to the same shape so they stay executable:

```mdx
## When to use this
One or two sentences on the trigger.

## Prerequisites
What must already be true or in hand.

## Steps
Numbered, in order, each one an action rather than a description.

## Definition of done
The observable state that means it worked.

## Failure modes
What usually goes wrong, and what to do instead.
```

## Wanted

Gaps we know about. Write one when you have run the thing enough times to know how it should go.

- Onboarding a new customer team after they sign
- Handling an inbound privacy or data-protection question
- Qualifying an inbound lead against the [ICP](/company/icp-and-market)
- Responding to a churn signal from a paying team
- Preparing an investor update
- Introducing a manager to the coaching playbook — who delivers it, and when in the customer lifecycle

<Tip>
  Do not write a playbook for something you have not done. A speculative playbook gets followed by someone who assumes it was tested.
</Tip>
