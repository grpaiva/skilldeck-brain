## The idea

Most development plans fail because they try to improve a person on several dimensions at once. Skill Deck does the opposite: **one Skill, for at least two weeks, with a Goal**.

A **Track** is that unit. A **Cycle** is a quarter, and a person works through a series of Tracks within it.

## Why one at a time

Two reasons, and both are worth giving to a skeptical buyer.

A person can only deliberately practice one behavior at a time. Splitting attention across five Skills produces attention on none.

And evidence is scarce. Scoring one Skill closely across two weeks of real meetings produces something specific enough to act on. Spreading the same evidence across the whole model produces a dashboard.

## Track lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="The Skill unlocks">
    A Skill becomes eligible once its confidence passes the unlock threshold, currently **0.30**. Before that, the starting level is a guess. See [Confidence and scoring](/product/confidence-and-scoring).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pending Skill Selection">
    Status `pending_attribute`. The Track exists but the Skill to focus on has not been settled yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pending Goal definition">
    Status `pending_goal`. The Skill is chosen; the Goal score is being set.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Active">
    Status `active`. The Track is running on the Advisor stage ladder, every new piece of evidence is read against this Skill, and stage threads go out by email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It ends">
    A Track reaches one of three terminal states: `completed` when the member finishes the last stage (apply the chosen Practice with an outcome); `switched` when the person moves to a different Skill; or `expired` when it goes stale. It can also be **rolled** into a fresh Track at a Cycle boundary.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  A Track left in a pending state is the most common stuck state a manager will see. The Manager Coaching Playbook is explicit: do not leave a Skill or Goal pending across a Weekly coaching meeting, and prefer auto-assignment at the Monthly and Quarterly levels so a member is never blocked waiting on you.
</Warning>

## The Advisor stage ladder

While a Track is active, the member moves through a fixed ladder of stages with the Advisor. Each stage has a clear goal. Conversation stages advance when the member replies substantively by email or in the app. Meeting stages advance when the meeting bot sees the required behavior in a real meeting.

| Position | Stage | Completes by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Discuss the Skill with your Advisor | Conversation — a substantive reply about the Skill |
| 2 | Bring the Skill into a meeting | Meeting — meaningfully mention the Skill |
| 3 | Choose your Practice | Conversation — pick a Practice and confirm it |
| 4 | Talk about your Practice in a meeting | Meeting — talk about the chosen Practice |
| 5 | Apply your Practice | Meeting — apply the Practice with a visible outcome |

Opening the Track sends a Kickoff that starts stage 1. Completing stage 5 completes the Track. Stages 2, 4, and 5 wait on a verifier that reads the meeting through the stage goal before the analysis email lands. That verifier's rationale and next step address the member as "you".

Kickoff includes an **Advisor Picks** episode on the Track Skill. The episode teaches that Skill for the member's role and recent work — about three to five minutes — using the member's profile, Deck, latest evaluations, and latest evidences. The Kickoff email carries a written version of the teaching before the listen link, so the member can read it without playing the audio. See the [glossary](/overview/glossary).

A **Practice** is a named behavior from the Skill's level catalog — the concrete move the member chooses to work on, drawn from options above their current score. Stage 3 lists those options, then adds exactly two sentences before the required closing: these Practices were selected so the member can improve or go deeper on the Skill (saying how many are listed), and the one they choose is the Practice they will now go deep on. The closing asks them to reply with the one they want; after they choose, the Advisor sends an episode they can read or listen to, then they confirm together. Completing that choice generates the Practice episode before the stage 4 opening is sent, so the opening can carry the episode summary. That episode teaches the chosen Practice in the member's context: when the Practice is itself a tool (a named method, framework, or template), it explains how the tool works; otherwise it may use one or two tools already named in the member's context. Stage 4's opening explains the chosen Practice in two or three sentences using only the definition and example already on file, shows that written summary, connects the summary to talking about the Practice in the meeting, and still includes the listen link. The spoken script stays out of the email.

Stage openings, Advisor dialogue, the stage verifier, and Track episodes all receive the same member context: profile, Deck, the ten latest evaluations, and the five latest evidences. Stage-specific opening briefs live on each stage record. For meeting stages, the opening prompt still carries the meeting-bot completion rules.

Each stage that opens a conversation gets its **own email thread**. Subjects include the Skill or Practice name so different Tracks do not group together in the inbox. When a conversation stage is fulfilled, that thread closes and the next stage opens as a new email. Meeting-stage openings mention the meeting bot and do not ask for a Journal write-up.

In the app, the member (and a manager viewing them) opens the Track page: **Overview** for stage progress, and **Conversations** for the Advisor threads. Managers read those threads read-only; only the member can reply.

A global kill-switch (`TRACKS_STAGED_V1`) gates the ladder. Production turns it on after active Tracks are onboarded onto stages. While it is on, an active Track with a stage completes through Stage 5 rather than through Friday score-Goal checks.

### Timing rules

| Rule | Value | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum duration | **~2 weeks** (14 days) | Behavior change needs more than one good meeting. Weekly Prep and Review still follow the working week. |
| Stale expiry | **90 days** without score movement | Prevents a disengaged user from being stuck on a Track they will never finish. |
| Inactivity pause | **7 days** without any evaluation | Track communications and recurring jobs are skipped until the person is back. |

That last rule matters for the product's tone. Someone on holiday or in a heads-down month does not come back to two weeks of accumulated nagging.

### Goals

A Goal is a whole number on the 0–20 scale, normally the next integer above the current score, and **never below level 5**. When the product sets it automatically the formula is `min(max(floor(score) + 1, 5), 20)`.

The Goal still frames where the person is aiming and which Practice options sit above their score. On the staged ladder, finishing the Track means completing Stage 5, not waiting for a Friday Goal check.

## Do not churn Skills

<Warning>
  **Switching Skills so often that Tracks never reach meaningful practice is the single biggest failure mode in how Skill Deck gets used.** The Manager Coaching Playbook names it at every management level and builds its default bias against it: keep the Track unless there is a strong reason to switch.
</Warning>

The reasoning is worth internalising, because the instinct runs the other way. A Track sitting at flat progress looks like a Track that is not working, and the most available action is to change the Skill. But a long Track with no movement is usually an evidence problem — the person is not in the situations where that Skill shows up — and switching the Skill does not fix it. Diagnose evidence quality and situation fit first.

## The quarterly Cycle

Tracks sit inside a Cycle. Cycle dates can follow the calendar quarter or be custom, which is worth confirming before running a quarterly review.

At the end of a Cycle the product runs a sequence, in order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The next quarter is created">
    `quarters:create-next`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Snapshots are generated">
    `snapshots:generate-quarterly` captures each Deck at that point in time, which is what makes quarter-over-quarter change visible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reports are generated">
    `reports:generate-quarterly`. The report callout can appear on the Deck.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Active Tracks roll over">
    `tracks:roll-cycle` rolls an active Track into a fresh one when the user's Cycle ends.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A Track that rolls over at the Cycle boundary without finishing its stages is not treated as a failure. Its [Wrap-Up](/product/track-dispatches) is written as a retrospective — what was done, what improved, what to carry forward.

This is what gives a manager something concrete at the end of each quarter: a snapshot, a report, and a set of Track outcomes to review.

### How many Tracks fit in one

The count follows the work. Two weeks is the usual practice window and a Cycle is a quarter, so several short Tracks and a handful of longer ones are equally healthy shapes for the same quarter. A Cycle is judged on whether each Track produced practice a manager can point to.

Treat depth as the measure and let the count land where it lands. Managing to a Track count is the fastest route to [Skill churn](#do-not-churn-skills).

## Goals and Targets

A Track has a **Goal** score that frames the aim and the Practice options.

Separately, the product models optional **Targets** (`TargetType`), set over a period for a person or a team:

- **Skill target** — focused on specific Skill scores.
- **Confidence target** — reach a minimum average confidence across the whole Deck.

The confidence target is the interesting one for a new user or a new team. Early on, the most valuable goal is not "get better at X" but "give the product enough evidence that the profile means something".

## Resource cards

When a Track starts, the user draws a **hand of up to four resource cards**: the Goal-level behavior plus the three levels above it, each one a named, concrete behavior with a description and an example. They are told to play whichever card the situation calls for. Stage 3 asks them to **choose a Practice** from those options and confirm it with the Advisor.

Cards are the product's central metaphor made literal. A person given one target behavior fixates on it and misses richer openings. A person holding several named behaviors picks the one the moment actually calls for.

<Warning>
  Members see card **names**, never the level numbers behind them. The scores still matter — they set the Track Goal, and managers read them. Hide the number on the card so the person uses the behavior when the opening comes, not because it is a higher score. See [Track dispatches](/product/track-dispatches).
</Warning>

## Stage check after evaluation

After a meeting is evaluated, if the person has an **active Track**, Skill Deck settles that meeting for the Track. On meeting stages, a **stage check** reads the meeting against the stage goal before the analysis email lands. When the check is met, the stage advances. The verifier's rationale and next step address the member as "you". When the check has a rationale, next step, or excerpt, the evidence page **Analyses** tab shows a **Track analysis** card — Skill and stage under the title when available, badge **Stage met** or **Not yet**, those fields, a quoted excerpt when one exists (including unmet checks with an empty quote), and a **View Track** link to that Track. The same stage-check material appears in the [Evidence Evaluated](/product/track-dispatches) email.

Without an active Track, evaluation still scores the Deck — it just does not run a stage check. See [Evidence and evaluation](/product/evidence-and-evaluation).

## Enabling Tracks

Tracks are **live in production**. Teams turn them on per team (team page), along with automation settings and the whitelist. That per-team switch is step two of the Manager Coaching Playbook's six moves, and skipping it is the most common setup failure.

Do not describe Tracks as dark-launched or globally off. A global kill-switch still exists in application config for emergencies; production has it on. The staged ladder has its own kill-switch (`TRACKS_STAGED_V1`); production turns that on with the stage ship.
