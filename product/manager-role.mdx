## The position we take

Skill Deck does the analysis. The manager does the leading.

We are explicit, in the pitch and in the product, that the human role of the leader is essential to development. The tool is not trying to replace the manager with an algorithm — it is trying to remove the two things that stop managers from developing their people: no time, and no specifics.

The playbook states the division cleanly: the manager's job is **not to replace the product's weekly autopilot**. It is to coach, guide, and hold accountable.

## What a manager sees

Two surfaces.

### Team page

Two tabs.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Members** | The member list (add, remove, change role) and sub-teams. With Tracks on, the current Cycle's stats and the member Tracks timeline sit above the list. This is where stuck states show up: an empty member row, a Pending status, a member lagging peers on the timeline. |
| **Team settings** | The team's [Focus Skills](#focus-skills), with the company's shown read-only, and **Members can choose their own Focus Skills**. With Tracks on, Track Settings: enable Skill Tracks, and automation. |

### Member page

One person in depth. With Tracks off, the tabs are **Deck** (opened first), **Journal**, and **Settings**. With Tracks on, they are **Overview** (opened first), **Tracks** or **Journal**, **Deck**, and **Settings**. The playbook's standard prep order is **Overview → Tracks / Conversations → Deck**:

| Surface | What the manager reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Progress vs Goal, days or weeks on the current Track, Track history — completed, still active, switched, rolled, expired — and the Cycle's evaluation count |
| **Tracks** | The member's current and past Tracks. Open a Track for stage progress on **Overview**, and Advisor threads on **Conversations** (read-only — managers never reply or mark threads read). Meeting-evaluated threads carry stage-check outcomes for the one-meeting deep-dive |
| **Deck** | Current scores and confidence across the member's Skills, plus a callout that a quarterly report exists |
| **Journal** | The member's full Journal, including Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts. Opening a post leaves its read state unchanged for the member. Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts carry no action button, because the evidence page and the episode belong to the member |
| **Settings** | The member's Focus Skills, with team and company ones shown read-only, and, with Tracks on, per-member Track Settings |

<Warning>
  The manager's view stops there, by design and in code. They cannot open an individual evidence page or a transcript, the **Evidence Evaluated** email goes to the member alone, and they cannot open the body of the member's report. They read the member's Journal, including Evidence Evaluated posts with their Skill highlights and Feedback, and Track **Conversations** (including meeting-evaluated threads), read-only. A member still sees more about themselves than their manager does.

  See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data) before answering any question about this.
</Warning>

## What a manager can do

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Set up the team" icon="settings">
    On the team page's **Team settings** tab: choose the team's Focus Skills and, with Tracks on, enable Skill Tracks and set automation. This is step two of the playbook's six moves, and skipping it is the most common setup failure.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Choose Focus Skills" icon="list-filter">
    Pick the Skills that matter most for a person or a team. See [Focus Skills](#focus-skills).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Start, configure, edit, and end Tracks" icon="flag">
    Tracks are off in production as of October 2026 while they are redesigned. See [Cycles and Tracks](/product/cycles-and-tracks).

    A manager can start a Track, configure a pending Skill or Goal, and edit an active Track.

    To end an active Track early, use **End track** on the member's **Tracks** tab (beside **Open track**) or in the Track flyout on the team timeline. It asks for confirmation, closes the Track as `switched`, sends Wrap-Up, and starts the next Track with its own Kickoff. Managers manage their own Tracks the same way. See [Ending a Track early](/product/cycles-and-tracks#ending-a-track-early).

    They can also leave it to automation. The playbook recommends auto-assigning the Skill and Goal at the Monthly and Quarterly levels, so a member is never blocked waiting on a manager decision.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Set optional Targets" icon="target">
    Targets can be created for an individual member or a whole team. Two kinds: a **Skill target** on specific scores, or a **confidence target** to reach a minimum average Deck confidence.

    Targets are optional in the playbook's agendas. The confidence target is the right first one for a new team, because nothing can be developed until the profiles mean something.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Review at the end of a period" icon="calendar-check">
    At the end of each Track, month, or Cycle, a manager has data to review both how the tool is being used and how the person is developing. The Cycle-end pipeline generates snapshots and reports automatically, which is what makes quarter-over-quarter comparison possible.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Focus Skills

**Focus Skills** are the Skills that matter most for a person, matched to job scope, personal challenges, or organizational values. They can be set at three levels.

| Level | Where | Who sets them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | **Company settings** | Organization Owner or Admin |
| Team | The team page's **Team settings** tab | The team's managers, or an Owner or Admin |
| Person | The member page's **Settings** tab, or the person's own **Settings → Focus Skills** | A manager, or the person when allowed |

They resolve **person first**. A person's own Focus Skills win. Without them, the union of their teams' Focus Skills applies, and without those, the union of their active companies'. With none set anywhere, the person has no Focus Skills.

A person with no team and no active company always chooses their own. Inside a company, a person chooses their own when the company or one of their teams turns on **Members can choose their own Focus Skills**. That setting is off by default, and a team that turns it off blocks it for that company. Everyone else sees their Focus Skills read-only, labelled with the level that set them.

Focus Skills decide three things:

- Which Skills get [Feedback](/product/evidence-and-evaluation) after each meeting.
- Which Skill the weekly [Advisor Pick](/product/track-dispatches) episode is about.
- Which Skills a Track can start on, with Tracks on.

They work the same with Tracks on or off.

<Warning>
  Skill evaluation scores every Skill the meeting gave the member an opportunity to use, and the Deck shows every scored Skill. Focus Skills shape what Skill Deck coaches next. Evidence has to turn up on its own to count.
</Warning>

That detail is the answer to "is it just going to tell me what I want to hear?"

## The coaching cadence

A manager picks a **management level** — Weekly, Monthly, or Quarterly — based on team size, seniority, Skill criticality, and how mature the team's evidence is. Levels are named for how often the _manager_ meets, not for how often Skill Deck emails the member; the product still sends stage threads, Monday Prep, and Friday Review on its own schedule.

## One-to-ones

The coaching meeting _is_ the one-to-one, and it is where Skill Deck pays off: the manager arrives with stage progress, themes from the week's Prep, Review, and Advisor threads, and a specific meeting to walk through.

<Warning>
  There is **no dedicated 1-on-1 feature**. Sessions, agendas, and notes are not product objects. The playbook supplies the agenda; the product supplies the material.

  Describe the one-to-one as the use case the manager surfaces support, not as a module.
</Warning>

## The failure mode to design against

Across all three management levels, the playbook names the same one: **Skill churn**. Switching Skills so often that Tracks never reach the two-week completion window.

It is worth understanding why this is the recurring risk. A manager who is engaged wants to act, and the most available action is changing the Skill. The playbook's default bias — keep the Track unless there is a strong reason to switch — exists to redirect that energy into coaching practice quality instead.

## The limits

A manager's access is bounded by the product's privacy model, and those bounds are a selling point rather than a limitation.

- **No full transcripts.** After 7 days the meeting transcript is gone from cache. The owner's quotes behind Skill demonstrations and stage-check excerpts stay with those conclusions in the database.
- **Only the member's own behavior is evaluated.** A meeting produces evidence about the Skill Deck user it came from, not about their colleagues.

## Who counts as a manager

Access to a member's page requires one of:

- **Team Manager** on that member's team, or on any **ancestor team** — manager access cascades down to sub-teams.
- **Organization Owner or Admin**, who get manager-equivalent access across the organization.

There is no separate "direct report" object. Management is expressed through team membership plus the Manager role.

## What the member is told

The member learns the shape of manager access from the **User Guide**, opened from the sidebar and published at `/knowledge-base`. The disclosure ships in English, Portuguese, and Spanish.

The product draws the line this way. A manager can open the Deck and its confidence, Cycle progress, Tracks with their history, Focus Skills and Track Settings, Advisor conversations, and the member's Journal, including Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts. The evidence page (its Analyses and Scores tabs, with Feedback and Deep Dive), transcripts, and Advisor Picks audio stay with the member.

A member reads a superset of that on their own Overview, Deck, Journal, and Tracks, so a manager holds nothing the member has not already seen.

Every dispatch and stage thread is addressed to the member alone. A manager reads dispatches in the member's Journal and Conversations on the member's Tracks, both on the member page.

Access follows the roles above and applies the same way to everyone on a team. The boundary lives in the authorization layer rather than in a per-member setting, so "who can see my data" has one answer for the whole team.
