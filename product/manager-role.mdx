## The position we take

Skill Deck does the analysis. The manager does the leading.

We are explicit, in the pitch and in the product, that the human role of the leader is essential to development. The tool is not trying to replace the manager with an algorithm — it is trying to remove the two things that stop managers from developing their people: no time, and no specifics.

The playbook states the division cleanly: the manager's job is **not to replace the product's weekly autopilot**. It is to coach, guide, and hold accountable.

## What a manager sees

Two surfaces.

### Team page

Two tabs.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Members** | The member list (add, remove, change role) and sub-teams. The [Team score](#team-score-and-member-score) sits above the list. With Tracks on, the current Cycle's stats sit above the Team score and the member Tracks timeline below it. This is where stuck states show up: an empty member row, a Pending status, a member lagging peers on the timeline. A **1-on-1 ready** badge on a member's row links to their [1-on-1](#1-on-1-reports) tab when a report can be generated. |
| **Team settings** | The team's [Focus Skills](#focus-skills), with the company's shown read-only, and **Members can choose their own Focus Skills**. With Tracks on, Track Settings: enable Skill Tracks, and automation. |

### Member page

One person in depth. With Tracks off, the tabs are **Deck** (opened first), **Journal**, **1-on-1**, and **Settings**. With Tracks on, they are **Overview** (opened first), **Tracks** or **Journal**, **1-on-1**, **Deck**, and **Settings**. The **1-on-1** tab appears on the page of every member the leader manages, and never on the leader's own page. The playbook's standard prep order is **Deck → Focus Skills → Journal → Monthly Report**. The tabs:

| Surface | What the manager reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Progress vs Goal, days or weeks on the current Track, Track history — completed, still active, switched, rolled, expired — and the Cycle's evaluation count |
| **Tracks** | The member's current and past Tracks. Open a Track for stage progress on **Overview**, and Advisor threads on **Conversations** (read-only — managers never reply or mark threads read). Meeting-evaluated threads carry stage-check outcomes for the one-meeting deep-dive |
| **Deck** | The [Member score](#team-score-and-member-score) first, then current scores and confidence across the member's Skills, plus a callout that a quarterly report exists |
| **Journal** | The member's full Journal, including Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts. Opening a post leaves its read state unchanged for the member. Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts carry no action button, because the evidence page and the episode belong to the member |
| **1-on-1** | Period reports that prepare the leader's one-to-one with the member: a summary, period highlights with their evidence, suggested questions, a PDF, and an optional audio briefing. See [1-on-1 reports](#1-on-1-reports) |
| **Settings** | The member's Focus Skills, with team and company ones shown read-only, and, with Tracks on, per-member Track Settings |

<Warning>
  The manager's view stops there, by design and in code. They cannot open an individual evidence page or a transcript, the **Evidence Evaluated** email goes to the member alone, and they cannot open the body of the member's report. They read the member's Journal, including Evidence Evaluated posts with their Skill highlights and Feedback, and Track **Conversations** (including meeting-evaluated threads), read-only. A [1-on-1 report](#1-on-1-reports) reads scores and evaluation summaries, and its evidence links open the member's Journal. A member still sees more about themselves than their manager does.

  See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data) before answering any question about this.
</Warning>

### Team score and Member score

The Team score and the Member score show what kind of situations the evaluations came from. Each card has three tiles, **Pressure**, **Complexity**, and **Effect**. A tile gives the share of evaluations in the window that carried that [context bonus](/overview/glossary), the change in points against the window before, and the share of each kind of that bonus. Each tile stands on its own; the three are not parts of a total. An info button on each tile explains the bonus and its kinds.

| Card | Where | Covers | Reference marker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Team score** | Team page, **Members** tab | The team's direct members. The card says **Direct members only** when the team has sub-teams | The organization's share |
| **Member score** | Member page, top of the **Deck** tab | One member | The team's share |

A team counts each qualifying member equally, so one person with many meetings does not set the team's share alone. A member below the minimum sample stays out of the team's share. When the sample is too small, the card says there are not enough evaluations yet instead of showing a rate. The window and minimums are on [Canonical facts](/overview/canonical-facts).

Pressure and Complexity describe the situation, not the person. Read a change as a change in the meetings or in the behavior, and ask before concluding which.

Organization Owners and Admins also see the **Organization score** on the organization dashboard. See [Organizations and teams](/product/organizations-and-teams#organization-score).

    To end an active Track early, use **End track** on the member's **Tracks** tab (beside **Open track**) or in the Track flyout on the team timeline. ## Focus Skills

**Focus Skills** are the Skills that matter most for a person, matched to job scope, personal challenges, or organizational values. They can be set at three levels.

| Level | Where | Who sets them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | **Company settings** | Organization Owner or Admin |
| Team | The team page's **Team settings** tab | The team's managers, or an Owner or Admin |
| Person | The member page's **Settings** tab, or the person's own **Settings → Focus Skills** | A manager, or the person when allowed |

They resolve **person first**. A person's own Focus Skills win. Without them, the union of their teams' Focus Skills applies, and without those, the union of their active companies'. With none set anywhere, the person has no Focus Skills.

A person with no team and no active company always chooses their own. Inside a company, a person chooses their own when the company or one of their teams turns on **Members can choose their own Focus Skills**. That setting is off by default, and a team that turns it off blocks it for that company. Everyone else sees their Focus Skills read-only, labelled with the level that set them.

Focus Skills decide three things:

- Which Skills get [Feedback](/product/evidence-and-evaluation) after each meeting.
- Which Skill the weekly [Advisor Pick](/product/track-dispatches) episode is about.
- Which Skills a Track can start on, with Tracks on.

They work the same with Tracks on or off.

<Warning>
  Skill evaluation scores every Skill the meeting gave the member an opportunity to use, and the Deck shows every scored Skill. Focus Skills shape what Skill Deck coaches next. Evidence has to turn up on its own to count.
</Warning>

That detail is the answer to "is it just going to tell me what I want to hear?"

## The coaching cadence

The manager meets each person for **15 minutes, once a month**, on its own or as the last 15 minutes of a monthly 1:1. The cadence is the _manager's_, not how often Skill Deck emails the member. The product still sends its dispatches on its own schedule.

## One-to-ones

The coaching meeting _is_ the one-to-one, and it is where Skill Deck pays off. The manager arrives with the Deck read and a 1-on-1 report open, and goes through the period's findings. The playbook supplies the agenda; the product supplies the material.

## 1-on-1 reports

The member page's **1-on-1** tab prepares a leader for a one-to-one with that member. Anyone who can manage the member has it (see [Who counts as a manager](#who-counts-as-a-manager)), on every member except themselves. A report is generated only when a leader clicks **Generate 1-on-1 report**.

### When a report can be generated

| Report | When it opens | Period it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 30 days after the member's first evaluated meeting, with at least one evaluated meeting in the last 30 days | The last 30 days |
| Each next one | 30 days after the previous report's period ended, once a meeting has been evaluated since | From the day after the previous period ended to today. After more than 90 days, the last 30 days |

The tab says why a report is not available yet and, while the 30-day minimum runs, the date it opens. Days count in the leader's time zone. One report generates at a time for each member. Reports belong to the team: a member on two teams has separate reports on each team's member page.

### What a report holds

Generating creates a [Snapshot](/product/snapshots) of the member for the period. AI then writes the report in the leader's language, usually in under a minute.

| Part | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Summary** | An opening summary of the period |
| **Period strip** | Evaluated meetings and evaluations in the period, and the member's Focus Skills |
| **Period highlights** | 3 to 7 blocks picked from the period's data: Focus Skill, Most evaluated, Strength, Score gain, Attention point, Activity shift, Work setting (Pressure and Complexity), Meeting traction (Effect), and Follow-up on the previous report. Each block gives an observation and a **Hypothesis & conversation angle**, and lists up to three meetings as evidence: title, date, evaluation summary, and score. Each meeting links to its Evidence Evaluated post in the member's **Journal** tab |
| **Suggested questions** | 2 or 3 open questions to test the hypotheses. Each has a checkbox that stays ticked, and the list has a **Copy** button |

A selector switches between the member's reports. Every leader who can manage the member sees the same reports on that team, including ones another leader generated, and the same ticked questions.

**Download PDF** gives a one-page A4 guide laid out for the meeting, with space for next steps and notes. Japanese, Korean, and Chinese characters do not render in the PDF.

**Generate audio** records a spoken briefing of three to four minutes for the leader who asked for it. The briefing is that leader's own [Advisor Pick](/overview/glossary): it sits in their Advisor Picks feed with a 1-on-1 badge and a **View report** link. The member never receives it.

### What a report reads

A report reads scores, context bonus statistics, Focus Skills, evaluation summaries, and the member's previous 1-on-1 reports. It never reads a transcript or the quotes behind Skill demonstrations. Its evidence links open the member's Journal, never the evidence page. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data).

### The weekly reminder

On Mondays at 09:00 in the recipient's time zone, Skill Deck emails one reminder listing the members whose report can be generated, each with a link to their **1-on-1** tab.

- It goes to the team's direct Managers. A team with no direct Manager sends it to the organization's Owners and Admins.
- Only organizations with an active subscription receive it.
- A member appears in the first two weekly reminders after a report opens, then every 28 days until a leader generates one.
- A recipient never appears in their own reminder.

## The failure mode to design against

The playbook names one: **changing Focus Skills too often**. A skill that changes every month never gathers enough evidence to be worth reading.

It is worth understanding why this is the recurring risk. A manager who is engaged wants to act, and the most available action is changing the Skill. The playbook's default is to keep the Focus Skills most months, with two or three months on a skill as a reasonable floor. That redirects the energy into coaching practice quality instead.

## The limits

A manager's access is bounded by the product's privacy model, and those bounds are a selling point rather than a limitation.

- **No full transcripts.** After 7 days the meeting transcript is gone from cache. The owner's quotes behind Skill demonstrations and stage-check excerpts stay with those conclusions in the database.
- **Only the member's own behavior is evaluated.** A meeting produces evidence about the Skill Deck user it came from, not about their colleagues.

## Who counts as a manager

Access to a member's page requires one of:

- **Team Manager** on that member's team, or on any **ancestor team** — manager access cascades down to sub-teams.
- **Organization Owner or Admin**, who get manager-equivalent access across the organization.

There is no separate "direct report" object. Management is expressed through team membership plus the Manager role.

## What the member is told

The member learns the shape of manager access from the **User Guide**, opened from the sidebar and published at `/knowledge-base`. The disclosure ships in English, Portuguese, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, and Traditional Chinese.

The product draws the line this way. A manager can open the Deck and its confidence, Cycle progress, Tracks with their history, Focus Skills and Track Settings, Advisor conversations, and the member's Journal, including Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick posts. Leaders also generate 1-on-1 reports about the member, which only leaders open. The evidence page (its Analyses and Scores tabs, with Feedback and Deep Dive), transcripts, and Advisor Picks audio stay with the member.

A member reads a superset of that on their own Overview, Deck, Journal, and Tracks. The leader views on top of that are the Member score on the member page and the Team score on the team page. They count how often evaluations carried each context bonus, using the same evaluations that build the member's own Deck.

Every dispatch and stage thread is addressed to the member alone. A manager reads dispatches in the member's Journal and Conversations on the member's Tracks, both on the member page.

Access follows the roles above and applies the same way to everyone on a team. The boundary lives in the authorization layer rather than in a per-member setting, so "who can see my data" has one answer for the whole team.
