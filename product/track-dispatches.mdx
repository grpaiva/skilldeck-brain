Skill Deck delivers itself by email. This is the low-friction claim in practice: a user connects a calendar and then receives everything in their inbox, without logging in.

## How Track mail works now

An active Track runs on the **Advisor stage ladder**. Each conversation stage opens as its **own email thread**. The member replies in that thread (or in Conversations in the app). When the stage is fulfilled, the Advisor closes the thread and the next stage opens as a new email. Meeting stages open with coaching that mentions the meeting bot; they complete from real meetings, not from a Journal write-up.

Thread subjects name the **Skill** or **Practice** so two Tracks on the same stage do not collapse into one conversation in the inbox.

Weekly Prep and Weekly Review still run on the working week. Evidence Evaluated still lands after a meeting is analyzed. Completing the last stage ends the Track, sends Wrap-Up, and starts the next Track; Wrap-Up stays open for a reply and does not name the next Skill.

<Note>
  Dispatches and stage threads keep running at this rhythm regardless of how often a manager meets their team. The Manager Coaching Playbook's Weekly, Monthly, and Quarterly levels describe the _manager's_ cadence — the product still emails the member on its own schedule.
</Note>

In the app, stage mail and replies live on the Track page under **Conversations**. Soft Skill highlights stay with the member.

## Stage openings and threads

| Stage | Opens as | Advances when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Discuss the Skill | Kickoff / stage-opening email | Member sends a substantive reply about the Skill |
| 2 · Bring the Skill into a meeting | Stage-opening email | Meeting bot sees a meaningful mention of the Skill |
| 3 · Choose your Practice | Stage-opening email | Member picks a Practice and confirms it |
| 4 · Talk about your Practice | Stage-opening email | Meeting bot sees talk about that Practice |
| 5 · Apply your Practice | Stage-opening email | Meeting bot sees the Practice applied with an outcome |

Completing stage 5 completes the Track and triggers Wrap-Up.

Stage 3 opening lists the Practice options, then adds exactly two sentences before the required closing: these Practices were selected so the member can improve or go deeper on the Skill (saying how many are listed), and the one they choose is the Practice they will now go deep on. The closing asks them to reply with the one they want; after they choose, the Advisor sends an episode they can **read or listen to**, then they confirm together.

Completing the Practice choice generates the Practice episode before the stage 4 opening is sent, so that opening can carry the episode gist. That episode teaches the chosen Practice in the member's context — about three to five minutes — and explains a tool when the Practice is one, or may use one or two tools already named in the member's context. While the stage 4 opening is still pending, the standalone Practice written-preview email is suppressed; otherwise that preview still posts on the choose-Practice thread with a listen link.

Stage 4 opening explains the chosen Practice in two or three sentences using only the definition and example already on file, shows the Practice episode written summary, connects that summary to talking about the Practice in the meeting, and adds the listen link. The spoken script stays out of the email.

Stage openings, Advisor dialogue, the stage verifier, and Track episodes receive the member's profile, Deck, ten latest evaluations, and five latest evidences. Stage-specific opening briefs live on each stage record. Meeting-stage openings still carry the meeting-bot completion rules in the opening prompt.

While replying on a conversation stage, the Advisor can also call the same tools as the Skill Deck MCP server, scoped to that Track member — Skill scales before explaining a score, cached meeting detail before quoting a transcript, Advisor Picks or Targets when the member asks, and launching the meeting bot to a Zoom, Microsoft Teams, or Google Meet link they provide. Tool results do not complete a stage. See [Cycles and Tracks](/product/cycles-and-tracks) and [Syncing from production](/overview/syncing-from-production).

Inbound replies use a Mailgun webhook on the Advisor mailbox (`TRACKS_INBOUND_ADDRESS` with a plus-token). Only the Track owner can reply; managers read Conversations read-only.

## The scheduled dispatches

| Dispatch | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kickoff** | When a Track starts (including right after the previous Track's last stage) | Opens the Track and stage 1; carries that Track's instructions |
| **Weekly Prep** | Mondays, 12:30 | Coaches the current stage from Track progress and meeting stage checks |
| **Evidence Evaluated** | After a meeting is analyzed | The analysis of that meeting |
| **Weekly Review** | Fridays, 16:00 | Reviews the week against the current stage; does not complete the Track |
| **Wrap-Up** | When a Track ends (last stage completed, or switched) | Reviews only the Track that ended; stays open for a reply |

On the staged ladder, Friday Review and the quarter Cycle roll leave an active Track in place. Completing Stage 5 is what ends the Track and starts the next one.

## Resource cards

Before the individual messages make sense, this concept has to be clear, because it shapes all of them.

When a user starts a Track, they draw a **hand of resource cards** — the Goal-level behavior plus the levels above it, each one a concrete behavior with a name, a description, and an example of what it looks like. Stage 3 asks them to choose one as their **Practice**.

<Warning>
  **Members never see the level number behind a card.** Refer to a card by name. The scores still matter — they set the Track Goal, and managers read them on the Overview and the Deck. Hide the number on the card so the person uses the behavior when the opening comes, not because it is a higher score.

  Pitch and demo copy may still quote a numeric level. Do not describe the emails as delivering scores.
</Warning>

The design reasoning is worth repeating in a demo: a person given a single target behavior fixates on it and misses richer openings. A person holding several named behaviors picks the one the moment calls for.

## Each scheduled dispatch

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Kickoff" icon="flag">
    Sent when a Track starts or when an active Track is onboarded onto the ladder. It opens stage 1 and can replace the Monday Weekly Prep for that week. When the previous Track ended on Stage 5, Kickoff goes out with the new Track right away — Wrap-Up does not carry the next Skill.

    It orients the user: you are starting a Track on this Skill, here is where you stand today, grounded in what your recent meetings actually show. It walks through the hand of cards by name, then asks one reflection question that starts the Advisor conversation.

    When a Skill episode is ready, Kickoff includes a **written version** of that Advisor Picks episode — three to five short paragraphs that teach the same idea as the audio — then the listen link. The episode teaches the Track Skill for the member's role and recent work, grounded in their profile, Deck, latest evaluations, and latest evidences. It runs about three to five minutes. The stage-opening writer pins that written version and link; the composing agent does not summarize the episode itself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Weekly Prep" icon="calendar">
    Mondays at 12:30, for users with an open Track.

    On an active staged Track, Prep coaches the **current stage** from Track progress and meeting stage checks — stage feedback from this week's window first, earlier checks as background. When a Practice is chosen, it talks about that Practice by name. It does not organize the week as a hand of cards or as moving a score toward a Goal.

    The Advisor Picks episode for the week is generated at 11:00, an hour and a half earlier, so Prep can reference the week's learning topic and link a fresh episode when the meetings actually touched it.

    Weekly comms adapt to where the user is (`WeeklyTrackCommsMode`): `active` and `pending` are Track-bound, while `unlocking` and `ready_to_start` cover people who do not have a Track yet — someone still accumulating evidence gets a different message from someone mid-Track.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Evidence Evaluated" icon="file-check">
    Sent after a meeting is evaluated. This is the "analysis after every meeting" from the pitch. Subject: `Meeting analysis for :title`.

    The body is **static**, assembled from stored data. When a stage check exists for that meeting, the email includes whether the stage was met, the rationale and next step (addressing the member as "you"), and an italicized excerpt when the check has one. When the check is **Not yet**, the next step names a moment from that meeting and gives one example sentence the member could have said there, using details from the transcript — not a generic Practice checklist. When the member has an active Track, it can also show the tracked Skill score from that meeting. It then shows the stored **Top Strength** and **Growth Opportunity** Soft Skill highlights — summaries already written in the member's language.

    When Skill Deck can attach the meeting to a Track, the email joins that Track's Advisor thread — Advisor From and Reply-To — and appears under **Conversations**. Attachment prefers the Track from the latest stage check for that meeting, then the member's active Track. A stage check alone is enough; a written analysis is not required. Soft Skill highlights stay with the member. Managers read the meeting-evaluated Conversations thread read-only and do not reopen the member-only Journal Evidence Evaluated post.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Weekly Review" icon="chart-bar">
    Fridays at 16:00. On an active staged Track, Review looks at the week against the **current stage** and the same stage-progress / stage-check material as Prep. It does not complete the Track, name a next Skill, or frame the week as a score moving toward a Goal.

    Completing Stage 5 is what ends the Track. Friday Review leaves the active Track in place.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wrap-Up" icon="trophy">
    Sent when a Track ends — usually because Stage 5 finished, and also when the Track was switched. It can land on any day.

    It reviews **only the Track that just ended**: the path through the stages, the chosen Practice by name, and meeting stage feedback on file. The member can reply on that thread. It does not name, suggest, or invent a next Skill. A new Track starts on its own, and its Kickoff carries that Track's instructions.

    When the Track ends without finishing Stage 5, the Wrap-Up is written as a retrospective rather than a failure.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How messages are written

Kickoff, stage openings, Prep, Review, Wrap-Up, and Advisor replies are composed by AI agents from the real Track data — stage progress, stage checks, the chosen Practice, cards where they still apply, and member context — in the user's language. If AI composition is off or the call fails, scheduled dispatches fall back to static translated templates where those exist. `COMMS_AI_ENABLED` defaults to on.

**Evidence Evaluated is never AI-composed.** It renders the stage check, Soft Skill highlights, and scores.

### The rules the coach writes under

These are in the prompt guidelines, and they tell you a lot about the product's intended voice:

- Warm, direct, coach-like. No corporate filler, no exclamation overload.
- Concrete and grounded in the data provided. **Never invent meetings, scores, quotes, or events.** If context is thin, keep the message short rather than padding it.
- Written entirely in the user's language — English, Spanish, or Brazilian Portuguese — with no English product terms leaking into a translated body.
- Refer to cards and Practices by name, never by level or score.

<Note>
  "If context is thin, keep the message short instead of padding it" is a good line to quote when someone asks whether this is just AI-generated noise. The product is built to say less when it has seen less.
</Note>

## When messages stop

A user with no evaluation for **7 days** is treated as inactive, and Track communications and recurring Track jobs are skipped until they are back. Someone on holiday does not return to a fortnight of accumulated email.

<Tip>
  For a manager, paused emails are a signal rather than a quiet failure. "Weekly emails may pause" is listed as a red flag in the Manager Coaching Playbook, and the intervention is upstream: fix integrations and bot coverage, and agree on concrete meetings to capture.
</Tip>
