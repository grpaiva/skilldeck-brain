## Confidence, in one sentence

Confidence says **how much evidence stands behind a score**, on a scale from 0 to 1.

This is the concept people most often get wrong, so be precise about it:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="What low confidence means" icon="check">
    We have not observed this Skill enough times to say much about it yet.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What it does not mean" icon="x">
    That the person is inconsistent, unreliable, or that the score is inaccurate.
  </Card>
</Columns>

Never describe confidence as "accuracy" or "certainty". Confidence is a statement about our evidence, not about the person and not about whether we got it right.

### How it is shown

| Confidence | Colour | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50% and above | Green | Reliable |
| 30–49% | Amber | Moderate |
| Below 30% | Red | Low |

The Deck has a **reliable only** toggle that hides anything below 30%, treating it like an unscored Skill. That threshold is the same one that gates Track unlocking, which is why the Manager Coaching Playbook has managers check "Skills with confidence over 30%".

## What moves confidence

Four factors combine.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How many evaluations exist" icon="hash">
    The base factor. Confidence climbs toward its maximum as evaluations accumulate, on a curve that rises quickly at first and then flattens.

    The target for maximum base confidence is **20 evaluations** for a Skill (`CONFIDENCE_TARGET_EVALUATIONS`), with a curve steepness of 3.0 so that early evidence counts for a lot.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How recent that evidence is" icon="clock">
    Old evidence carries less weight. Confidence decays with a **half-life of 180 days**, and evaluations from the **last 30 days** get a weight bonus of 2.0.

    A person who was observed heavily last year and not at all since will see confidence fall, which is the correct behavior: we no longer know whether that is still true of them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What kind of evidence it is" icon="layers">
    Each evidence type carries a multiplier. Real work — meetings, email, conversations, calls, documents, files — all count at 1.0.

    An `activity_session` counts at **0.8**. A practice exercise is still evidence, but it is simulated rather than observed, and the product is explicit about valuing it less.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The bounds" icon="brackets">
    Confidence is clamped between **0.01 and 1.00**. It never reaches zero and never quite implies certainty.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Category-level confidence includes Skills that have no scores at all, so a category is not credited for Skills the product has never observed.

## Why confidence gates development

A Skill becomes eligible for a [Track](/product/cycles-and-tracks) once its confidence passes the **unlock threshold**, currently **0.30**.

The reasoning is worth understanding because it comes up in demos. Developing a Skill requires knowing where the person is starting from. Below the threshold, the starting level is a guess, so the Track would be built on nothing.

The Manager Coaching Playbook has managers work with this number directly: in the Weekly prep checklist, they check the Skills with confidence over 30% around the Track Skill.

<Note>
  The threshold sits at 30% deliberately, to give new users faster traction. The application's own configuration notes that the ideal long-term value is 50%. Expect this number to move.
</Note>

## How a level is derived

A person's level in a Skill is not the average of their evaluations.

**Time-based exponential decay** is the active algorithm (`time_based_exponential_decay`). Each evaluation is weighted by its actual date on a 180-day half-life, so recent behavior dominates and older behavior fades. This is what makes the profile a picture of who someone is *now* rather than a running total of everything they have ever done.

Two older algorithms exist in the code — a simple average and a batch-based exponential decay — but they are legacy. Do not describe them as options.

<Note>
  **Scores are decimals; levels are integers.** A person's score is a weighted mean carried to one decimal place, so someone can sit at 12.3 — between the level 12 and level 13 behaviors. The 21 written level descriptors are the integer rungs; the score is a position on the ladder, not a rung.

  Track Goals are always whole numbers, and never below level 5.
</Note>

Category scores are a weighted average of the Skills in that category, and **only Skills that have been scored are included**. A category is never dragged down by Skills the product has not observed.

### Top Performance Average

Alongside the current level, the product computes a **Top Performance Average**: the average of a person's best scores rather than all of them.

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Share of top scores included | 5% |
| Minimum evaluations before the percentage applies | 5 |
| Minimum top scores always included | 1 |

Below five evaluations, all scores are used. The purpose is to capture what someone is capable of at their best, which is a different and useful question from what they do on average.

## Explaining this to a customer

Three points, in this order:

1. **Confidence is about us, not about you.** It measures how much we have seen.
2. **Recent behavior counts more.** The profile tracks who you are now, and it decays if we stop seeing you.
3. **Development waits for evidence.** We do not put you on a Track for a Skill we have barely observed, because we would be guessing at your starting point.

That third point turns the product's slowness into a statement of rigor, which is what it actually is.

## Hollow confidence

The Manager Coaching Playbook has a useful phrase for the failure state: **confidence that is not hollow** means scores backed by recent evidence across the quarter.

A Deck can look complete while resting on evidence that is months old and decaying. Falling confidence with flat progress is the signal, and the fix is never a new Goal — it is calendar connection, bot coverage, and getting the person into the meetings where the Skill shows up.
