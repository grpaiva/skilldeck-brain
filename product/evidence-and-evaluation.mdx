## What evidence is

Evidence is a piece of real work that Skill Deck analyzes. It is the foundation of the entire product claim: we evaluate what people did, not what they or anyone else says about it.

Evidence volume is something a manager is expected to watch — "What got evaluated?" opens the Weekly coaching meeting.

| Status | Source | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live, first-run** | Meetings the bot attends | The source we sell and onboard on. Connect a calendar. |
| **Live, not first-run** | Slack conversations | Implemented. Do not ask new customers or test teams to connect Slack at the start. Slack evaluations tend to score lower than meetings. |
| **Never implemented** | Email, documents, files, calls | Early PRD only, same vintage as Activities. Do not claim them. |
| **On hold** | Other messaging (Teams chat and similar) | Roadmapped, then paused after Slack scores came in lower than meetings. Do not promise them. |

The enum still lists `email`, `phone_call`, `document`, `file`, `chat_topic`, and `activity_session`. Those names are leftover. The live claim is **real work evidence from meetings**, not AI interviews and not a multi-channel inbox.

`activity_session` is leftover from the unused [Activities](/product/cycles-and-tracks) feature. If one ever scored, it would count at a 0.8 confidence multiplier — do not treat that as a current product surface.

## From meeting to evidence

<Steps>
  <Step title="The calendar integration schedules a bot">
    Connecting Google Calendar or Microsoft Calendar automatically schedules **transcript-only** meeting bots for upcoming meetings. For Zoom, the bot can join as the user using an OnBehalf token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The bot joins">
    The bot's progress is visible in the evidence status: `waiting_for_meeting`, `bot_joining`, `bot_in_waiting_room`, then `recording` — shown to the user as **In Meeting**.

    A bot sitting in a waiting room is a visible state, not a silent one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transcription and speaker mapping">
    Status moves through `transcribing` and `mapping_speakers`. Speaker mapping is what makes the rest possible: without knowing who said what, nothing can be attributed to a person.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Processing and deduplication">
    The content is processed into evidence. A duplicate detector runs, and repeated content is marked `duplicate` rather than scored twice — so a meeting attended by several Skill Deck users does not inflate anyone's evidence count.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Triage">
    A triage agent reads the evidence and decides **which Skills it can say anything about**. A one-hour meeting speaks to a handful of Skills, not to all 58.

    If the person's manager has whitelisted Skills for them, those are passed into triage as a mild bias — but only when the person has no active Track. See [The manager role](/product/manager-role).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluation">
    For each Skill triage selected, an evaluator scores the observed behavior against that Skill's level scale, with a written justification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scores update">
    Status reaches `evaluated`, scores and confidence move, and an [Evidence Evaluated dispatch](/product/track-dispatches) goes out.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The gate: silence is not a score

This is the most important design decision in the evaluation pipeline, and it is a strong point in a demo.

When an evaluator returns a near-zero score, a separate gate agent inspects the justification and decides whether it should be recorded at all. The rule it applies:

- If the justification says the evidence contained **no clear, direct, relevant behavior** for that Skill, the evaluation is discarded.
- If it describes **relevant behavior, even very poor behavior**, the evaluation is kept.

A person is not marked down for a Skill that simply did not come up. A quiet meeting is not a bad score — it produces no score. Genuinely poor behavior does get recorded, including a zero.

This is what stops the profile drifting toward the mean as evidence accumulates, and it is the honest answer to "does it penalize me for meetings where I just listened?"

## What an evaluation contains

Each evaluation carries a score against the Skill's level scale and a written justification tied to what was actually observed.

While a [Track](/product/cycles-and-tracks) is running, the evidence is also mined for **moments**: up to five short excerpts per piece of evidence, each labelled a **demonstration** or an **opportunity**. Excerpts are capped at 500 characters, which limits how much transcript content the product persists.

Together these form the **Track analysis** for that meeting, which is what a manager walks through in a coaching deep-dive.

## Asking questions about your evidence

The product includes agents for generating questions about a piece of evidence and answering them, so a user can interrogate their own analysis rather than only receive it. This powers the "Deep Dive" experience on evidence details.
