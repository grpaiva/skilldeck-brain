## What evidence is

Evidence is a piece of real work that Skill Deck analyzes. It is the foundation of the entire product claim: we evaluate what people did, not what they or anyone else says about it.

Evidence volume is something a manager is expected to watch — "What got evaluated?" opens the Weekly coaching meeting.

The live claim is **real work evidence**. See [Messaging and claims](/playbooks/messaging-and-claims) for the claims that are off limits.

## Where evidence comes from

Evidence has **two** sources.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Meeting bots" icon="video">
    A transcript-only bot attends the meeting on **Google Meet**, **Zoom**, or **Microsoft Teams**. This is the first-run source and the one we sell on.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Slack" icon="message-square">
    For the channels the user selects, our bot reads messages daily and evaluates them. Implemented, not first-run.
  </Card>
</Columns>

### The connections that feed them

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Google Calendar or Microsoft Calendar" icon="calendar">
    Connecting a calendar is what makes bots join **automatically**. Skill Deck reads upcoming events, and schedules a bot for any event whose meeting link points at Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams.

    The calendar's job is deciding which meetings get a bot.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Zoom" icon="video">
    Zoom needs **its own connection** on top of the calendar. Once connected, the bot joins as the user, which is what lets a scheduled Zoom meeting be covered.

    Zoom must also grant the bot permission to capture. If that never happens, the meeting ends without a transcript — see [When a meeting does not produce evidence](#when-a-meeting-does-not-produce-evidence).

    The product emails users who have upcoming Zoom meetings and no Zoom connection. Confirm the connection before a pilot with a Zoom-first team.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Slack" icon="message-square">
    A custom integration with a second step: after connecting the workspace, the user **picks the channels** to track. Channel selection keeps the scope to what the user chose to share.

    A daily sync then reads new messages in each selected channel and turns them into evidence, which goes through the same Skill evaluation as a meeting.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  Do not ask new customers or test teams to connect Slack at the start. Slack evaluations tend to score lower than meetings. Other chat platforms, such as Teams chat, are on hold for the same reason.
</Note>

## From meeting to evidence

<Steps>
  <Step title="A bot gets scheduled">
    A connected calendar schedules a **transcript-only** bot for any upcoming event whose link points at Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams. Zoom also needs its own connection, so the bot can join as the user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The bot joins">
    The bot's progress is visible in the evidence status: `waiting_for_meeting`, `bot_joining`, `bot_in_waiting_room`, then `recording` — shown to the user as **In Meeting**.

    A bot sitting in a waiting room is a state the user can see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attendee returns the transcript">
    [Attendee](/overview/glossary) transcribes the meeting and maps speakers. Skill Deck receives text that already says who said what.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Processing">
    The transcript is processed into evidence, then Skill evaluation runs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One evaluator per Skill">
    Every Skill that a transcript can show gets its own evaluator, and each evaluator reads the whole meeting. Written Communication and Nonverbal Communication need a written or nonverbal record, so meetings are scored on the other Skills. See [Skills and levels](/product/skills-and-levels).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Moments">
    The evaluator finds each **moment** where the meeting gave the evidence owner a real opportunity to use that Skill: something they did in the meeting, or a case they told about having lived. It places each moment on one of the five [stages](/product/skills-and-levels#levels), notes which context bonuses apply, and quotes the owner's own words.

    A Skill can have **several moments** in one meeting. A Skill the meeting gave no opportunity for has none — see [No opportunity, no score](#no-opportunity-no-score).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Quotes and scores">
    Skill Deck checks each quote against the owner's speaking turns. A harmful-behavior moment counts only when the transcript names its speakers and the quote is the owner's.

    Skill Deck, not the model, computes every score: the stage's base plus 1 for each context bonus present, or 0 for a harmful behavior. Each scored moment is a **demonstration** of that Skill.

    Each Skill's methodology lists up to four harmful behaviors, or none, and the evaluator considers only the behaviors listed. For a Skill with none, the evaluator is told the Skill has no harmful behaviors, every moment lands on a stage, and Skill Deck drops any harm moment reported for it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Summaries and highlights">
    Each demonstration gets a short summary, and each Skill gets a summary of how it showed up across the meeting. Then Skill **highlights** are chosen and stored: a **Top Strength**, and a **Growth Opportunity** when more than one Skill was demonstrated. A Skill with a harmful behavior is the Top Strength only when it is the one Skill demonstrated, and it comes first for the Growth Opportunity.

    Summaries are written in the **evidence owner's language** — English, Spanish, or Brazilian Portuguese — from their account language setting. They address the subject as "you", open on the action or observation, and describe what happened without naming a stage or a score. They do not restate the Skill name — the card already shows it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Feedback">
    When the evidence owner has [Focus Skills](/product/manager-role#focus-skills) and at least one of them has a moment above 0 in the meeting, the meeting gets one **Feedback** paragraph. The writer reads every such Focus Skill and chooses the one where this meeting gives the clearest next step; on a tie, the Skill with more moments. A paragraph about any other Skill or moment is rejected, and the attempt is retried. The paragraph is written to the owner as "you", in their language: what they did in one moment, one step further described as a behavior, and an experiment in the form "when [trigger], do [action]" with a new example sentence they could say.

    The step aims at the stage above that Skill's average in this meeting and draws on the Skill's practice catalog without naming the practice. The tone is positive. Scores can appear; stage names do not. Feedback runs after highlights, independently of the Deep Dive, and a Feedback failure never holds up the evidence. Paragraph length is on [Canonical facts](/overview/canonical-facts).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scores update">
    Every demonstration moves the person's score for that Skill, in the order the moments happened, and writes its own score history. Status reaches `evaluated` once highlights are stored and Feedback has run — or immediately when the meeting gave no Skill an opportunity. The evidence counts once toward each evaluated Skill's evidence count on the Deck.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence Evaluated email">
    A meeting with demonstrations sends the [Evidence Evaluated](/product/track-dispatches) email. It is assembled from stored data: the meeting title and date, how many Skills were evaluated and their overall average, then the Top Strength and Growth Opportunity, each with the Skill and its summary. When no highlights were stored, it shows the three Skills with the highest averages. It then shows the Best Moment, the meeting's highest-scoring demonstration with its summary, and the Most Demonstrated Skill, the Skill with the most demonstrations when it has at least two, with the count and its average. When Feedback was written, the email carries a Feedback section with that paragraph and its Skill. The same content is stored as the member's Journal entry, which the member's managers can read on the member page.

    Tracks are off in production as of October 2026 while they are redesigned, so the email goes out as a Skill Deck system email. With Tracks on, it joins the member's Track thread and carries the stage check.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When a meeting does not produce evidence

Not every bot session becomes scored evidence.

| Case | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Single-participant meeting** | Marked processed without transcription or evaluation. There is no behavior with others to observe. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data). |
| **Evidence owner never speaks** | The transcript names its speakers and none of them is the evidence owner. Marked processed with no scores, highlights, or Evidence Evaluated email. Skill Deck matches the owner by comparing each speaker label with the account's first name; a label that carries more than the first name must also contain the last name. When that check finds no match, a fast model can still map a shorter compound name, a surname, an initial, or a nickname to the owner. Labels the model invents are ignored. If that mapping step fails, evaluation retries instead of treating the owner as absent. The same resolved labels decide which speaking turns the quotes are checked against. A transcript without speaker labels is evaluated as usual, with quotes checked against the whole transcript. |
| **No usable transcript** | For example the Zoom bot joined but never received external-recording permission, or Attendee ended without a transcript after retries. Evidence is marked failed. Meeting-recording quota is released. Skill Deck does **not** send an error email. |
| **Real processing error** | Evidence is marked failed and the user gets an error notification. |

## No opportunity, no score

This is the most important design decision in Skill evaluation, and it is a strong point in a demo.

A Skill is scored from a meeting only at the moments where the meeting gave the person a real opportunity to use it. Each Skill's methodology defines what opens that opportunity. A Skill the meeting gave no opportunity for gets no score from it, and its Deck score stays where it was.

When an opportunity comes, the person's response lands on a stage. The lowest stage, Novice (1–4), is a real stage: the person acted, following a rule or a script. A harmful behavior scores 0, and only when the transcript shows one of that Skill's listed harmful behaviors with certainty. When in doubt, it is not harm. A Skill that lists no harmful behaviors never scores 0.

This is what keeps the profile tied to what the person actually did, and it is the honest answer to "does it penalize me for meetings where I just listened?" The Skills a meeting gave you no opening for stay exactly where they were.

## What an evaluated piece of evidence contains

On the evidence page that belongs to the person the evidence is about.

### Skill highlights

Stored for that evidence after Skill evaluation.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Top Strength** | The Skill this meeting showed at its best, with a short holistic summary in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and opens on the action |
| **Growth Opportunity** | A different Skill worth attention next, when more than one Skill was demonstrated |

Highlight cards show the Skill name and summary. They do not show a score pill. The summary does not restate the Skill name. When only one Skill was demonstrated, Top Strength is that Skill and Growth Opportunity is omitted.

### Analyses and Scores

The evidence page has two tabs and opens on **Analyses**.

| Tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Analyses** | In order: the **Feedback** card when the meeting has Feedback, with the Skill name and its paragraph; the **Track analysis** card when the meeting has a Track stage check (see [Stage check](#stage-check)); the Skill highlights; then **Deep Dive**, suggested questions about the meeting. The person clicks a question and Skill Deck answers it. |
| **Scores** | The Skill cards, with their sort menu and side panel. |

### Skill cards and demonstrations

The **Scores** tab heads the Skill cards with a count, for example "5 Skills evaluated". Each Skill that was demonstrated gets one summary card.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Demonstrations observed** | How many moments of that Skill were scored on this evidence |
| **Score or Average** | A score badge on the 0–20 scale. With one demonstration it is that score; with several it is the average, followed by the highest and lowest scores |
| **Scale markers** | Dots on the 0–20 scale for the distinct scores on this evidence, in the shared score colors |
| **Summary** | How the Skill showed up across the meeting, in the evidence owner's language |
| **Harmful behavior** | A red badge when a demonstration recorded a harmful behavior (score 0) |
| **See score / See all scores** | Opens a side panel for that Skill |

When more than one Skill was demonstrated, a sort menu orders the cards by highest average (the default), lowest average, highest score, lowest score, or most demonstrations. Ties fall back to the highest average, then the Skill name.

The side panel repeats the stats and scale with the full Skill summary, then lists every score for that Skill from highest to lowest. Each entry shows the score badge, a harmful-behavior badge when it applies, that demonstration's summary, and the Deck movement that score made.

Cards and panels show scores. Stage names stay with the methodology and the AI tools.

A **demonstration** is one scored moment of that Skill. Each demonstration writes its own score history and counts toward that Skill's confidence. The Deck counts this evidence **once** in that Skill's evidence count, however many demonstrations it produced. The evidence list shows how many Skills each piece of evidence evaluated.

### Stage check

Stage checks are part of Tracks, which are off in production as of October 2026 while they are redesigned. With Tracks on, a verifier reads the meeting through the stage goal on meeting stages of an active Track, before the Evidence Evaluated email lands.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill and stage** | Named under the **Track analysis** title when available (Skill name · Stage N · stage title) |
| **Stage met / Not yet** | Whether the stage goal was satisfied in that meeting |
| **Rationale and next step** | Written to the member as "you". When the check is **Not yet**, the next step names a moment from that meeting and gives one example sentence the member could have said there, using details from the transcript — not a generic Practice checklist. When the check is **Stage met**, the next step is one short sentence. |
| **Excerpt** | Optional meeting quote; empty when the check is **Not yet** |
| **View Track** | Ghost button in the card footer that opens that Track |

When any of those fields has text, the **Analyses** tab shows them under **Track analysis** — including an unmet check with an empty quote. The card names the Skill and stage when available, and **View Track** opens that Track. The viewer's open Track is preferred when more than one check is on file. The same met/not-yet, rationale, next step, and excerpt appear in the Evidence Evaluated email when a stage check exists.

A met check advances the Track stage.

<Note>
  The evidence page, with its Feedback, Skill cards, and Deep Dive, belongs to the person the evidence is about. Scores reach a manager through the member's Deck, and the Evidence Evaluated post through the member's Journal on the member page. Managers read meeting-evaluated **Conversations** threads read-only. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data).
</Note>

This material also carries into the member's Evidence Evaluated email and a manager's coaching preparation.
