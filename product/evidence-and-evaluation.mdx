## What evidence is

Evidence is a piece of real work that Skill Deck analyzes. It is the foundation of the entire product claim: we evaluate what people did, not what they or anyone else says about it.

Evidence volume is something a manager is expected to watch — "What got evaluated?" opens the Weekly coaching meeting.

The live claim is **real work evidence**. See [Messaging and claims](/playbooks/messaging-and-claims) for the claims that are off limits.

## Where evidence comes from

Evidence has **two** sources.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Meeting bots" icon="video">
    A transcript-only bot attends the meeting on **Google Meet**, **Zoom**, or **Microsoft Teams**. This is the first-run source and the one we sell on.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Slack" icon="message-square">
    For the channels the user selects, our bot reads messages daily and evaluates them. Implemented, not first-run.
  </Card>
</Columns>

### The connections that feed them

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Google Calendar or Microsoft Calendar" icon="calendar">
    Connecting a calendar is what makes bots join **automatically**. Skill Deck reads upcoming events, and schedules a bot for any event whose meeting link points at Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams.

    The calendar's job is deciding which meetings get a bot.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Zoom" icon="video">
    Zoom needs **its own connection** on top of the calendar. Once connected, the bot joins as the user, which is what lets a scheduled Zoom meeting be covered.

    Zoom must also grant the bot permission to capture. If that never happens, the meeting ends without a transcript — see [When a meeting does not produce evidence](#when-a-meeting-does-not-produce-evidence).

    The product emails users who have upcoming Zoom meetings and no Zoom connection. Confirm the connection before a pilot with a Zoom-first team.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Slack" icon="message-square">
    A custom integration with a second step: after connecting the workspace, the user **picks the channels** to track. Channel selection keeps the scope to what the user chose to share.

    A daily sync then reads new messages in each selected channel and turns them into evidence, which goes through the same Soft Skill evaluation as a meeting.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  Do not ask new customers or test teams to connect Slack at the start. Slack evaluations tend to score lower than meetings. Other chat platforms, such as Teams chat, are on hold for the same reason.
</Note>

## From meeting to evidence

<Steps>
  <Step title="A bot gets scheduled">
    A connected calendar schedules a **transcript-only** bot for any upcoming event whose link points at Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams. Zoom also needs its own connection, so the bot can join as the user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The bot joins">
    The bot's progress is visible in the evidence status: `waiting_for_meeting`, `bot_joining`, `bot_in_waiting_room`, then `recording` — shown to the user as **In Meeting**.

    A bot sitting in a waiting room is a state the user can see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attendee returns the transcript">
    [Attendee](/overview/glossary) transcribes the meeting and maps speakers. Skill Deck receives text that already says who said what.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Processing">
    The transcript is processed into evidence, then Soft Skill evaluation runs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Subject clips">
    The transcript is split into **subject clips** — short spans that keep the evidence owner's speaking turns in focus, with enough interlocutor context to read the moment. A short meeting may pass through as a single clip.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Behavior classification">
    Each clip is classified against the Skill level rubric. The classifier asks whether specific level behaviors appear in that clip, then aggregates hits into **demonstrations**: one scored observation of a Skill in that clip, tied to a level title on the 0–20 scale.

    A Skill can produce **several demonstrations** from one piece of evidence when the person shows that Skill more than once. Skills with no hits stay silent — see [Silence is not a score](#silence-is-not-a-score).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Justifications and highlights">
    Each demonstration gets a written justification tied to the clip. Each demonstration's subject-clip text is stored with the evaluation, so the justification can be written after the content cache expires. Then Soft Skill **highlights** are chosen and stored: a **Top Strength**, and a **Growth Opportunity** when more than one Skill was demonstrated. Highlight copy describes the Skill as a whole in the meeting, not a single demonstration's score.

    Justifications and highlight summaries are written in the **evidence owner's language** — English, Spanish, or Brazilian Portuguese — from their account language setting. They address the subject as "you", open on the action or observation, and do not restate the Skill name — the card already shows it. When a Skill name or level title appears later in that prose, it uses the same language.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scores update">
    Status reaches `evaluated` only after highlights settle — or immediately when Soft Skill evaluation produced no evaluations at all. Each demonstration updates score history for that Skill. Deck impact for a Skill on this evidence is aggregated across its demonstrations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stage check, if a Track is active">
    If the person's team has Tracks enabled and they have an **active Track**, a follow-on job settles the meeting for that Track. On meeting stages, a verifier runs a **stage check** against the stage goal. A met check advances the stage.

    If there is no active Track, this step is skipped.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence Evaluated email">
    After the Track settle finishes — or immediately after evaluation when there is no active Track — a meeting with evaluations sends the [Evidence Evaluated](/product/track-dispatches) dispatch. That email is assembled from the stage check when one exists, Soft Skill highlights, and scores.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When a meeting does not produce evidence

Not every bot session becomes scored evidence.

| Case | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Single-participant meeting** | Marked processed without transcription or evaluation. There is no behavior with others to observe. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data). |
| **No usable transcript** | For example the Zoom bot joined but never received external-recording permission, or Attendee ended without a transcript after retries. Evidence is marked failed. Meeting-recording quota is released. Skill Deck does **not** send an error email. |
| **Real processing error** | Evidence is marked failed and the user gets an error notification. |

## Silence is not a score

This is the most important design decision in Soft Skill evaluation, and it is a strong point in a demo.

A Skill is scored from this evidence only when the classifier finds **clear, demonstrated behavior** for that Skill. Skills that simply did not show up produce no demonstrations and no score movement.

A quiet meeting is not a bad score — it produces no score for the Skills that stayed silent. Genuinely poor behavior that the rubric does catch does get recorded, including a low or zero level.

This is what stops the profile drifting toward the mean as evidence accumulates, and it is the honest answer to "does it penalize me for meetings where I just listened?"

## What an evaluated piece of evidence contains

On the evidence page that belongs to the person the evidence is about.

### Soft Skill highlights

Stored for that evidence after Soft Skill evaluation.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Top Strength** | The Skill this meeting showed at its best, with a short holistic summary in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and opens on the action |
| **Growth Opportunity** | A different Skill worth attention next, when more than one Skill was demonstrated |

Highlight cards show the Skill name and summary. They do not show a score pill. The summary does not restate the Skill name. When only one Skill was demonstrated, Top Strength is that Skill and Growth Opportunity is omitted.

### Analyses and Deep Dive

The evidence page has two tabs.

| Tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Analyses** | When a stage check has a rationale, next step, or excerpt, a **Track analysis** card appears first: a subheading with the Skill name and stage (for example Skill name · Stage N · stage title) when that context is available, badge **Stage met** or **Not yet** in the card header, then the rationale and next step, and a quoted excerpt when one exists — including an unmet check with an empty quote. A ghost **View Track** button in the card footer opens that Track. Soft Skill highlights and Skill score cards follow. |
| **Deep Dive** | Suggested questions about the evidence. The person clicks one and Skill Deck answers it. |

### Skill cards and demonstrations

Each Skill that was demonstrated gets a card on the **Analyses** tab.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Demonstration** | One scored observation of that Skill in a subject clip: level title, score, justification in the evidence owner's language (second person, no Skill-name opening), and a clip excerpt while the content cache still holds it |
| **Scale markers** | Dots on the 0–20 scale for the distinct scores shown on this evidence; on multi-demo cards the dots are clickable to jump the carousel |
| **Carousel** | When a Skill has more than one demonstration, slides run low → high and open on the highest score |
| **Deck impact** | One aggregated score movement for that Skill from this evidence, shown under the card |

### Stage check

On meeting stages of an active Track, a verifier reads the meeting through the stage goal before the Evidence Evaluated email lands.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill and stage** | Named under the **Track analysis** title when available (Skill name · Stage N · stage title) |
| **Stage met / Not yet** | Whether the stage goal was satisfied in that meeting |
| **Rationale and next step** | Written to the member as "you" |
| **Excerpt** | Optional meeting quote |
| **View Track** | Ghost button in the card footer that opens that Track |

When any of those fields has text, the **Analyses** tab shows them under **Track analysis** — including an unmet check with an empty quote. The card names the Skill and stage when available, and **View Track** opens that Track. The viewer's open Track is preferred when more than one check is on file. The same met/not-yet, rationale, next step, and excerpt appear in the Evidence Evaluated email when a stage check exists.

A met check advances the Track stage.

<Note>
  Soft Skill evidence cards, highlights, the Deep Dive, and the evidence page stay with the person the evidence is about. Scores reach a manager through the member's Deck. Managers read meeting-evaluated **Conversations** threads read-only. See [Privacy and data](/product/privacy-and-data).
</Note>

This material also carries into the member's Evidence Evaluated email and a manager's coaching preparation.
