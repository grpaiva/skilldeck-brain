The Skill model is the product's core asset. It is what turns a transcript into something a person can act on, and it is the hardest part to copy.

<Note>
  **Skill** is the product term, in every language. `Attribute` is the internal database name for the same object and appears only in code and engineering docs.
</Note>

## Structure

```text
Category          8 of them
└── Skill         one behavior, e.g. Storytelling — can belong to several categories
    └── Level     0 to 20, each a named behavior with a worked example
```

<Warning>
  **A Skill can sit in more than one category.** This is the detail most people get wrong, including our own earlier documentation.

  Active Listening belongs to Communication, Interpersonal Relations, _and_ Leadership. Conflict Management belongs to Emotional Intelligence, Interpersonal Relations, and Leadership. Thirteen Skills are shared this way, so the per-category counts below add up to more than 58.

  When you count Skills, count unique Skills. The number is **58**.
</Warning>

## Levels

Each Skill is scored on a **0 to 20 scale**, and every point on that scale is a named, observable behavior with a worked example. This granularity is what the whole pitch rests on.

Each level has three parts:

| Part | Example, at Storytelling level 9 |
| --- | --- |
| **A name** | Narrative Pacing |
| **A behavior** | Consciously alternates between tension and relief, using pauses to let important narrative moments sink in |
| **An example** | The Rhythm-Keeper — "We pushed the code. We waited. (Long pause). And then, for the first time in three years... the system held." |

Those names are the [resource cards](/product/cycles-and-tracks) a member holds during a Track. The name is what they see; the number stays behind it.

<Note>
  Always say **21 levels** or **0 to 20**. Never "20 levels", and never "out of 20 levels".
</Note>

The bottom of the scale is not "not very good". It describes actively harmful behavior — level 0 of Storytelling is _Narrative Sabotage_, level 1 is _Toxic Framing_. The scale then climbs through disorganized, to competent, to behavior that changes an organization.

## How levels reach the user

Level numbers matter. They are the basis for a Track **Goal**, and managers read progress against them on the Overview and the Deck.

When those same levels are presented as **resource cards**, the number is hidden. The person should use the named behavior when the opening comes, not reach for a card because it is a higher score.

Pitch and demo copy may quote a numeric level — "You scored 8/20 in Storytelling" is accurate and approved. Member-facing cards and dispatch copy refer to cards by name.

## What is scored, and what is not

Skills carry `do_not_evaluate_on` rules that exclude them from evidence types where they cannot fairly be judged. A Skill that only makes sense in writing is not scored from a meeting transcript. Email evaluation was never implemented, so those Skills wait for a source we actually have.

Triage then narrows further: for each piece of evidence, it decides which Skills that evidence can meaningfully speak to. One meeting touches a handful, never all 58. See [Evidence and evaluation](/product/evidence-and-evaluation).

## Why the model is the moat

Worth understanding before you pitch it.

The models that read the transcripts are commodity. The integration that puts a bot in a meeting is buildable. What is not quickly reproducible is 58 Skills × 21 levels of written, observable behavioral descriptors, tuned so a language model can consistently place a person on the scale from evidence.

That is the asset. Lead with the granularity it produces, not with the AI.
