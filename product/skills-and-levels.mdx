The Skill model is the product's core asset. It is what turns a transcript into something a person can act on, and it is the hardest part to copy.

<Note>
  **Skill** is the product term, in every language. `Attribute` is the internal database name for the same object and appears only in code and engineering docs.
</Note>

## Structure

```text
Category          8 of them
└── Skill         one behavior, e.g. Storytelling — can belong to several categories
    └── Level     0 to 20: five stages of four points each, and 0 for a harmful behavior
```

<Warning>
  **A Skill can sit in more than one category.** This is the detail most people get wrong, including our own earlier documentation.

  Active Listening belongs to Communication, Interpersonal Relations, _and_ Leadership. Conflict Management belongs to Emotional Intelligence, Interpersonal Relations, and Leadership. Thirteen Skills are shared this way, so the per-category counts below add up to more than 58.

  When you count Skills, count unique Skills. The number is **58**.
</Warning>

## Levels

Each Skill is scored on a **0 to 20 scale**. Every Skill uses the same five stages, so a score means the same thing on every Skill: a 9 in Storytelling and a 9 in Delegation are both Competent.

The stages follow the Dreyfus model of skill acquisition, as Benner applied it to professionals in real work. Each stage adds one thing to the stage below, and that addition is the same for every Skill.

| Stage | Score band | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Novice** | 1–4 | The person acts, following a rule or a script, the same whatever the situation. The floor of every opportunity. |
| **Beginner** | 5–8 | Reading the situation. The response changes with what happened, but gives every element the same weight. |
| **Competent** | 9–12 | Salience and ownership. The person picks what matters most, says the reasoning out loud, and owns the move with a name and a date. |
| **Proficient** | 13–16 | Recognition. The person sees the whole situation, answers what is behind the words, and points out what departs from the expected. |
| **Specialist** | 17–20 | Fluid decision. The right move comes when the conversation calls for it; in a novel case the person says where the usual rule stops applying and adjusts. Rare. |

A moment's score is its stage's base — 1, 5, 9, 13, or 17 — plus 1 for each **context bonus** present.

| Bonus | Present when |
| --- | --- |
| **Pressure** | The situation weighs on the person when they act: something is lost or gained, someone with power is present or many people watch, they are criticized or blamed, or the answer must come now. |
| **Complexity** | What the person responds to is harder than the common case: it contradicts something said earlier, arrives in scattered parts, involves their own work or interest, or they lack authority, information, or decision power. |
| **Effect** | Later in the same meeting, others build on the move, the conversation changes course, or it becomes a decision or a task with an owner. |

**0 is a harmful behavior.** Each Skill lists four harmful behaviors. A moment scores 0 only when the transcript shows one of them with certainty and leaves no doubt about who spoke. Weak performance stays on a stage.

<Note>
  Say **0 to 20**. Never "20 levels", and never "out of 20 levels". Write a score as "8/20 in Storytelling".
</Note>

### What a Skill's methodology holds

Every Skill carries the same parts:

- **A definition** of the Skill's act, and whose move it is.
- **When the meeting asks for it** — the situation that opens an opportunity to use the Skill.
- **One core sentence per stage** — that stage's addition, applied to this Skill's act.
- **Four harmful behaviors**, each scoring 0.

The methodology is authored in Brazilian Portuguese, and the evaluator reads it verbatim. The Skill Deck MCP tool `get_attribute_scale` returns a Skill's definition, stages, context bonuses, and harmful behaviors.

### What the stages look like

Storytelling's stage cores, translated from its methodology entry on 2026-10-21.

| Stage | Storytelling |
| --- | --- |
| **Novice** (1–4) | Tells the case, even out of order, without landing the point, or as a list of facts with no why. |
| **Beginner** (5–8) | Grounds the story in the facts of this case: who, where, and when, in the order it happened, with the concrete detail that makes the case visible. |
| **Competent** (9–12) | Builds the story around the point the conversation needs: cuts what does not serve it, marks the turn, and closes by saying what the case means for the matter at hand. |
| **Proficient** (13–16) | Chooses and frames the case by what lies behind the conversation: names a concern someone showed, or the listener's own experience, and ties the case to it. |
| **Specialist** (17–20) | Works on the whole group: when the conversation is split or stuck, uses the case to give the group a shared reading of the situation and says what that reading changes for the team or the company. When the usual case does not fit, says where it stops fitting and what that difference shows. |

One of Storytelling's four harmful behaviors is using a story to deflect responsibility for a serious error by unfairly blaming others.

Every stage is an observable act tied to the Skill, not an adjective. That is what makes a score checkable against a transcript, and what makes the next stage actionable.

## How scores reach the user

Members see scores on the 0–20 scale on their Deck, their Skill pages, and their evidence pages. Stage names stay with the methodology and the AI tools.

Pitch and demo copy may quote a score — "You scored 8/20 in Storytelling" is accurate and approved.

Track Goals are whole numbers on the same scale. Tracks are off in production as of October 2026 while they are redesigned. See [Cycles and Tracks](/product/cycles-and-tracks).

## What is scored

Each Skill lists the evidence types it can be judged on. A transcript carries no written or nonverbal record, so meetings are scored on every active Skill except Written Communication and Nonverbal Communication.

Within those, a Skill is scored only at the moments where the meeting gave the person an opportunity to use it. One meeting touches some Skills and leaves the rest where they were. See [Evidence and evaluation](/product/evidence-and-evaluation).

## Why the model is the moat

Worth understanding before you pitch it.

The models that read the transcripts are commodity. The integration that puts a bot in a meeting is buildable. What is not quickly reproducible is the methodology: for each of the 58 Skills, what opens an opportunity in a meeting, what each of the five stages adds to that Skill's act, and which behaviors are harmful — written so a language model can consistently place a moment on the scale and quote the words behind it.

That is the asset. Lead with the granularity it produces, not with the AI.
