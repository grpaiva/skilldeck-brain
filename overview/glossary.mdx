## The word that matters most

**Skill.** Always Skill.

Skill Deck measures **58 Skills**. A user works a **Track** on one **Skill**. The product, the emails, and the Manager Coaching Playbook all say Skill, and so do we — in every language.

| Language | Term |
| --- | --- |
| English | Skill |
| Portuguese (Brazil) | habilidade |
| Spanish | habilidad |

<Note>
  **Attribute** is the internal database name for a Skill. It appears in code, enums, and engineering docs. It never appears in customer-facing writing.
</Note>

## The Skill model

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Skill">
    One of the 58 behaviors Skill Deck measures. The product term, in the UI, in emails, and in everything we write.

    Internally the same object is called an `Attribute`. Do not say "competency", "trait", or "metric".

    If the localized term ever changes, it changes in the code and translations first, and then here.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Category">
    The grouping that Skills belong to. There are 8: Communication, Creativity, Critical Thinking, Emotional Intelligence, Flexibility, Interpersonal Relations, Leadership, and Productivity.

    A Skill can belong to **more than one** category — Active Listening is in Communication, Interpersonal Relations, and Leadership. So the per-category counts add up to more than 58. Count unique Skills.

    Do not say "pillar", "domain", or "bucket".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Level">
    The graded position a person holds within a single Skill. There are **21 levels**, scored **0 to 20**. Each level has a written description of the behavior it represents.

    Always say **21 levels** or **0 to 20**. Never "20 levels". Write a score as "8/20 in Storytelling", not "a rating" and not "a grade".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Deck">
    A person's full set of Skills taken together — their behavioral profile, and the product's namesake. Also the name of the page where a user sees it.

    "Deck movement" means change in scores over a period. "Deck confidence" means confidence across the whole profile rather than one Skill.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Snapshot">
    A point-in-time capture of a Deck, used to show how a profile changed over a period. Generated automatically at the end of each Cycle.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Benchmark">
    A comparison of a person or team's scores against a reference set.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Evidence and evaluation

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Evidence">
    A piece of real work that Skill Deck analyzes. **Meetings** are the live source we sell and onboard on. **Slack conversations** are implemented but we do not ask new customers or test teams to connect Slack at the start.

    Other messaging platforms are on hold.

    Say "evidence", not "recording", "data point", or "sample". The live claim is **real work evidence**. See [Messaging and claims](/playbooks/messaging-and-claims) for the claims that are off limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Soft Skill evaluation">
    The production pipeline that scores Skills from evidence: subject clips, behavior classification against the level rubric, demonstrations, justifications, then Soft Skill highlights. Justifications and Soft Skill highlight summaries are written in the evidence owner's language. Live in production.

    Customer-facing copy still says **Skill**. "Soft Skill evaluation" names the pipeline; it is not a second taxonomy beside the 58 Skills.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Subject clip">
    A short span of evidence focused on the owner's speaking turns, with enough interlocutor context to read the moment. Soft Skill evaluation classifies each clip; a short meeting may pass through as a single clip.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Demonstration">
    One scored observation of a Skill in a subject clip: a level title, a score on the 0–20 scale, a written justification in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and does not open with the Skill name, and a clip excerpt while content is still cached. One Skill can have several demonstrations on the same evidence.

    Not a Track **moment**. Moments are Track-only excerpts; demonstrations are Soft Skill evaluation rows.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Evaluation">
    The scored assessment produced from a piece of evidence. Under Soft Skill evaluation, each demonstration is stored as its own evaluation row, so one evidence can hold several evaluations for the same Skill.

    Not a "test", a "review", or an "assessment result".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Soft Skill highlight">
    A stored Top Strength or Growth Opportunity for one piece of evidence, chosen after Soft Skill evaluation. The highlight holds the Skill, a holistic summary in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and does not restate the Skill name (the card already shows it), and an optional featured demonstration. Highlight cards do not show a score pill.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Top Strength">
    The Soft Skill highlight for the Skill this meeting showed at its best. Always present when Soft Skill evaluation produced at least one demonstration.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Growth Opportunity">
    The Soft Skill highlight for a different Skill worth attention next. Present when more than one Skill was demonstrated on that evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Confidence">
    How much evidence stands behind a score, expressed from 0 to 1 and usually shown as a percentage. Low confidence means "we have not seen enough of this yet", **not** "we think this person is inconsistent".

    Never call it "accuracy", "certainty", or "reliability". Managers watch for **hollow confidence** — a score that is not backed by recent evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Track analysis">
    The per-meeting analysis produced **only while a Track is active**: a Skill-focused summary plus moments. Written after evaluation, once the transcript is still in cache. Used in the Evidence Evaluated email, Weekly Review, and coaching deep-dives. Starting a Track backfills the last 7 days of evaluated meetings.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Moment">
    A short, specific excerpt from one piece of evidence showing the tracked Skill in action. Written only while a Track is active. Two types (`TrackMomentType`): a **demonstration**, where the person showed the behavior, and an **opportunity**, where they could have and did not. May be attributed to one resource card. Empty is allowed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The development cycle

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Track">
    A development cycle focused on a single Skill. **Tracks need about two weeks minimum** and can run longer.

    Statuses (`TrackStatus`): `pending_attribute` (shown as Pending Skill Selection), `pending_goal`, `active`, `completed`, `switched`, `expired`. A Track can also be **rolled** into a fresh one at a Cycle boundary.

    When the Track is on the Advisor stage ladder, progress follows stages 0–5 instead of Friday score-goal completion. See **Stage** and **Practice**.

    Not a "course", "program", "module", or "sprint".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Goal">
    The score a Track is aiming at on the score-goal path. A Track can complete only when the Goal is met and the minimum duration has passed, checked on Fridays.

    On the Advisor stage ladder, Stage 5 verification is the completion path instead of the Friday Goal check.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cycle">
    A quarter. Tracks live inside a Cycle, and Cycle boundaries trigger snapshots, reports, and Track rolls. Cycle dates can follow the calendar quarter or be custom.

    Use "Cycle" and "quarter" interchangeably in prose; "Cycle" is the product term.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Churn (Skill churn)">
    Switching Skills so often that Tracks never reach the two-week completion window. The single biggest failure mode in manager usage, and named as such in the Manager Coaching Playbook.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dispatch">
    A scheduled communication sent to a user during a Track, normally by email. Five standing types exist (`DispatchType`): **Kickoff**, **Weekly Prep**, **Evidence Evaluated**, **Weekly Review**, and **Wrap-Up**. **Advisor** is a further type while the stage ladder is on, and a manager can read those messages in Journal.

    In manager-facing writing, use the short names: Prep, Review, Kickoff, Wrap-Up. Not "notification", "alert", or "digest".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Unlock">
    The point at which a Skill has enough confidence behind it to be eligible for a Track. "Unlocking" is the state a member is in while they accumulate evidence and no Skill is eligible yet.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Target">
    An optional goal set over a period, for a person or a team. Two types (`TargetType`): a **Skill target** on specific scores, or a **confidence target** to reach a minimum average Deck confidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Resource card">
    One of the named behaviors a user holds during a Track — the Goal-level behavior plus the levels above it. Each card has a name, a description, and an example. Users see card names, never the level numbers behind them. The number still sets the Track Goal; it is hidden on the card so the person uses the behavior when the opening comes, not because it is a higher score.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stage">
    A step on the Advisor stage ladder for one Track, numbered 0 to 5: Kickoff, Reflect, Mention, Choose, Discuss, Apply. Implemented in the application, not first-run until Tracks are onboarded onto stages. See [Canonical facts](/overview/canonical-facts).

    While it is on, a Track completes when Stage 5 verification says the member applied their chosen Practice with a visible outcome.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Practice">
    The named behavior a member chooses at Stage 3, taken from that Skill's level catalog. Later stages ask them to talk about it, then apply it. Overview shows the Practice title only after Stage 3. There is no Practice picker on Overview.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Advisor thread">
    One conversation on a Track. Email and the site Journal are two clients of the same thread. The member replies from the Journal composer or by email. A manager reads the thread in Journal — stage, Advisor messages, the member's replies, and Verifier excerpts — and does not send a reply.

    Advisor Picks, listed under Surfaces, are the weekly audio episodes. The Advisor thread is the Track conversation.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Verifier excerpt">
    A short quote the Track Verifier attaches when it checks Stage 2, Stage 4, or Stage 5 against a meeting. It appears in Journal on the Advisor thread. A manager can read it there. The member's evidence page stays owner-only.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Surfaces

The pages a manager and a member actually work in. Use these names in any instruction, because they match what is on screen.

| Surface | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Deck** | The person's full Skill profile, with scores and confidence |
| **Overview** | The Track-centric page. On the score-goal path: progress vs Goal, time on Track, Track history, and the resource-card hand. On a staged Track: a stages timeline; the Practice title appears after Stage 3. See [Staged Overview](/product/cycles-and-tracks#staged-overview). |
| **Journal** | The member's reading feed of stored dispatches, plus the Advisor thread when a Track is on the stage ladder. The member has a composer on that thread. A manager reads stage, Advisor messages, and Verifier excerpts, and does not reply. |
| **Team page** | Cycle stats and the member Tracks timeline for a team |
| **Member page** | One person, seen by their manager. Prep for coaching in the order Overview → Journal → Deck |
| **Track Settings** | Where Skill Tracks, automation, and the whitelist are configured |
| **Advisor Picks** | Audio episodes for the Track. On the stage ladder, Kickoff and Practice episodes are anchored on the Skill or Practice |
| **Currency** | Settings → Currency. A person pins USD, BRL, or automatic (location). Catalog prices and checkout follow that pin. A card issued in Brazil is charged in BRL. |

## Organizations and people

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Organization">
    The customer account. Holds the subscription, the members, and the teams.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team">
    A group of people inside an organization, with a manager. Skill Tracks, automation, and the whitelist are set up here.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Organization role">
    Account-level permissions (`OrganizationRole`): **Owner** has full control including ownership transfer; **Admin** manages members, teams, and settings; **Billing** manages billing and subscription only; **Member** uses the product without admin controls.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team role">
    Team-level permissions (`TeamRole`): **Manager** or **Member**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Whitelist">
    The set of Skills a manager marks as most relevant for a person or team, based on job scope, personal challenges, or organizational values. Keeps Track focus role-aligned.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Management level">
    How often a manager meets their team about development: **Weekly**, **Monthly**, or **Quarterly**. Named for the manager's coaching cadence, not for how often Skill Deck emails the member.

    See the [Manager Coaching Playbook](/playbooks/manager-coaching/overview).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Other terms you will meet

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Insight">
    An observation the product surfaces about a person or a team, derived from their accumulated evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Superpower">
    An insight highlighting a standout strength, shareable as a public page or image.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quota">
    The internal metering that bounds how much processing a plan gets.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Integration">
    A connected external account (`IntegrationPlatform`): Google Calendar, Microsoft Calendar, Zoom, or Slack.

    Each integration has its own job. Calendars schedule the bots; Zoom needs its own connection so a bot can join Zoom meetings; Slack needs the user to pick channels. The evidence itself comes from **meeting bots** and **Slack channels**. Do not ask new customers to connect Slack at the start.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Attendee">
    The third-party service that supplies the meeting bots, and that transcribes the meeting and maps speakers. Internal term — customers should hear "the Skill Deck meeting bot".
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Words we do not use

| Never | Why | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competency, attribute (externally) | The product says Skill | Skill |
| Surveillance, monitoring | Repeating the frame reinforces it, even in a denial | Describe what the product does |
| Grading, ranking, judging | Implies comparison between people and a verdict | Scoring, evaluating, levelling |
| AI-powered, powerful, seamless | Says nothing and reads as filler | Name the mechanism |
| Accuracy (about confidence) | Confidence is about evidence volume, not correctness | Confidence |
