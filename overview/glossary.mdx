## The word that matters most

**Skill.** Always Skill.

Skill Deck measures **58 Skills**. A user works a **Track** on one **Skill**. The product, the emails, and the Manager Coaching Playbook all say Skill, and so do we — in every language.

| Language | Term |
| --- | --- |
| English | Skill |
| Portuguese (Brazil) | habilidade |
| Spanish | habilidad |

<Note>
  **Attribute** is the internal database name for a Skill. It appears in code, enums, and engineering docs. It never appears in customer-facing writing.
</Note>

## The Skill model

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Skill">
    One of the 58 behaviors Skill Deck measures. The product term, in the UI, in emails, and in everything we write.

    Internally the same object is called an `Attribute`. Do not say "competency", "trait", or "metric".

    If the localized term ever changes, it changes in the code and translations first, and then here.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Category">
    The grouping that Skills belong to. There are 8: Communication, Creativity, Critical Thinking, Emotional Intelligence, Flexibility, Interpersonal Relations, Leadership, and Productivity.

    A Skill can belong to **more than one** category — Active Listening is in Communication, Interpersonal Relations, and Leadership. So the per-category counts add up to more than 58. Count unique Skills.

    Do not say "pillar", "domain", or "bucket".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Level">
    A person's position on a single Skill, scored **0 to 20**. Five **stages** set the base, and **context bonuses** add to it. 0 means a **harmful behavior**. See [Skills and levels](/product/skills-and-levels#levels).

    Always say **0 to 20**. Never "20 levels". Write a score as "8/20 in Storytelling", not "a rating" and not "a grade".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stage">
    One of the five steps every Skill shares on the 0 to 20 scale: **Novice** (1–4), **Beginner** (5–8), **Competent** (9–12), **Proficient** (13–16), and **Specialist** (17–20). Each stage adds one thing to the stage below, the same for every Skill, so a Competent score means the same thing on every Skill. See [Skills and levels](/product/skills-and-levels#levels).

    Stage names live in the methodology and the AI tools. People see scores. Track stages are a separate ladder; see [Cycles and Tracks](/product/cycles-and-tracks).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Context bonus">
    One point added to a moment's stage base for each of **Pressure**, **Complexity**, and **Effect** present in that moment. A stage's band holds its base plus up to three bonuses.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Harmful behavior">
    One of the behaviors a Skill lists as harm: four per Skill, or none. A moment that shows one scores **0**. A Skill with none never scores 0. It counts only when the transcript shows the behavior with certainty and leaves no doubt about who spoke. Weak performance stays on a stage.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Deck">
    A person's full set of Skills taken together — their behavioral profile, and the product's namesake. Also the name of the page where a user sees it.

    "Deck movement" means change in scores over a period. "Deck confidence" means confidence across the whole profile rather than one Skill.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Snapshot">
    A point-in-time capture of a Deck, used to show how a profile changed over a period. Generated automatically at the end of each Cycle.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Benchmark">
    A comparison of a person or team's scores against a reference set.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Evidence and evaluation

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Evidence">
    A piece of real work that Skill Deck analyzes. **Meetings** are the live source we sell and onboard on. **Slack conversations** are implemented but we do not ask new customers or test teams to connect Slack at the start.

    Other messaging platforms are on hold.

    Say "evidence", not "recording", "data point", or "sample". The live claim is **real work evidence**. See [Messaging and claims](/playbooks/messaging-and-claims) for the claims that are off limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Skill evaluation">
    The production pipeline that scores Skills from evidence. One evaluator per Skill reads the whole transcript, finds the moments where the person had a real opportunity to use that Skill, places each one on a stage with its context bonuses, and quotes the person's words. Skill Deck checks the quotes and computes every score. Then Skill highlights are chosen, and Feedback is written for the owner's Focus Skills. Summaries are written in the evidence owner's language.

    It scores the 58 Skills listed on [Skills and levels](/product/skills-and-levels). See [Evidence and evaluation](/product/evidence-and-evaluation).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Demonstration">
    One scored moment of a Skill in a piece of evidence: a point where the meeting gave the evidence owner an opportunity to use that Skill, and what they did with it. It holds a score on the 0–20 scale and a short summary in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and does not open with the Skill name. One Skill can have several demonstrations on the same evidence. Engineering docs call a demonstration a **moment**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Evaluation">
    The scored assessment produced from a piece of evidence. Each demonstration is stored as its own evaluation, so one evidence can hold several evaluations for the same Skill, and each one moves that Skill's score.

    Not a "test", a "review", or an "assessment result".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Skill highlight">
    A stored Top Strength or Growth Opportunity for one piece of evidence, chosen after Skill evaluation. The highlight holds the Skill, a holistic summary in the evidence owner's language that addresses them as "you" and does not restate the Skill name (the card already shows it), and an optional featured demonstration. Highlight cards do not show a score pill.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Feedback (meeting)">
    One paragraph per Focus Skill after a meeting, written to the evidence owner as "you" in their language: what they did in one moment, one step further, and an experiment in the form "when [trigger], do [action]" with a new example sentence. It aims at the stage above the Skill's average in that meeting. It is the first tab on the evidence page and a section of the Evidence Evaluated email. Feedback is also the name of a Skill; say "meeting Feedback" when the two could be confused. See [Evidence and evaluation](/product/evidence-and-evaluation).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Top Strength">
    The Skill highlight for the Skill this meeting showed at its best. Always present when Skill evaluation produced at least one demonstration.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Growth Opportunity">
    The Skill highlight for a different Skill worth attention next. Present when more than one Skill was demonstrated on that evidence. A Skill with a harmful behavior comes first.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Confidence">
    How much evidence stands behind a score, expressed from 0 to 1 and usually shown as a percentage. Low confidence means "we have not seen enough of this yet", **not** "we think this person is inconsistent".

    Never call it "accuracy", "certainty", or "reliability". Managers watch for **hollow confidence** — a score that is not backed by recent evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stage check">
    Part of Tracks. On meeting stages of an active Track, a verifier reads the meeting against the stage goal. It records whether the goal was met, a rationale and next step that address the member as "you", and an optional meeting excerpt (empty when the goal was not met). When the check is not met, the next step names a moment from that meeting and gives one example sentence the member could have said there, using details from the transcript — not a generic Practice checklist. A met check advances the stage. The result appears in the Evidence Evaluated email and on the evidence page under **Analyses** as **Track analysis**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Track analysis">
    The product heading on the evidence page **Analyses** tab for the stage check when a rationale, next step, or excerpt exists. The card shows the Skill and stage under the title when available, **Stage met** or **Not yet** as a badge, then those fields, and a **View Track** link to that Track. Skill highlights and Skill cards sit below it on the same tab.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The development cycle

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Track">
    A development cycle focused on a single Skill. **Tracks need about two weeks minimum** and can run longer. Tracks are off in production as of October 2026 while they are redesigned; see [Cycles and Tracks](/product/cycles-and-tracks).

    Statuses (`TrackStatus`): `pending_attribute` (shown as Pending Skill Selection), `pending_goal`, `active`, `completed`, `switched`, `expired`. On the staged ladder, completing Stage 5 marks the Track completed and starts the next Track. **End track** closes an active Track early as `switched` and starts the next Track the same way. Friday Review and the Cycle roll leave an active Track in place.

    Not a "course", "program", "module", or "sprint".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Goal">
    The score a Track is aiming at. It frames where the person is heading and which Practice options sit above their score. On the staged ladder, finishing the Track means completing Stage 5, not waiting for a Friday Goal check.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cycle">
    A quarter. Tracks live inside a Cycle. Cycle boundaries trigger snapshots and reports. While staged Tracks are on, the Cycle roll does not close or replace an active Track. Cycle dates can follow the calendar quarter or be custom.

    Use "Cycle" and "quarter" interchangeably in prose; "Cycle" is the product term.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Churn (Skill churn)">
    Switching Skills so often that Tracks never reach the two-week completion window. The single biggest failure mode in manager usage, and named as such in the Manager Coaching Playbook.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dispatch">
    A scheduled communication sent to a user, normally by email: the Track mail, the post-meeting analysis, and the weekly Advisor Pick. Six types exist (`DispatchType`): **Kickoff**, **Weekly Prep**, **Evidence Evaluated**, **Weekly Review**, **Wrap-Up**, and **Advisor Pick**. On a staged Track, Prep and Review coach the current stage. Wrap-Up reviews only the Track that ended and does not name the next Skill. Stage openings and Advisor replies also travel as email threads on the Track page under **Conversations**.

    While Tracks are off, Evidence Evaluated and Advisor Pick are the dispatches that go out, as Skill Deck system emails.

    In manager-facing writing, use the short names: Prep, Review, Kickoff, Wrap-Up, Advisor Pick, and stage threads. Not "notification", "alert", or "digest".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Conversations">
    The Track-page tab that holds Advisor email threads for that Track — stage openings, replies, meeting analysis, and related thread types. The member replies there or by email; managers read read-only. While the Advisor replies, it can use the same member-scoped tools as the Skill Deck MCP server — Deck and evidence lookups, Skill scales, Advisor Picks, Targets, and launching the meeting bot when the member asks. Tool use does not complete a stage.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Unlock">
    The point at which a Skill has enough confidence behind it to be eligible for a Track. "Unlocking" is the state a member is in while they accumulate evidence and no Skill is eligible yet.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Target">
    An optional goal set over a period, for a person or a team. Two types (`TargetType`): a **Skill target** on specific scores, or a **confidence target** to reach a minimum average Deck confidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Resource card">
    One of the named behaviors a user holds during a Track — the Goal-level behavior plus the levels above it. Each card has a name, a description, and an example. Users see card names, never the level numbers behind them. The number still sets the Track Goal; it is hidden on the card so the person uses the behavior when the opening comes, not because it is a higher score.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Advisor Picks">
    Private audio episodes for the member. A **weekly episode** teaches one Skill and one practice from that Skill's practice catalog, about five and a half minutes long, on one of the person's Focus Skills when they have any. It reviews what the person does well on that Skill in recent meetings, then teaches the practice as one step-by-step for a meeting this week. It arrives as the **Advisor Pick** dispatch, or through Weekly Prep for people who get Prep. An episode generated on demand is written the same way.

    **Track episodes** are written for the Track. A Track episode runs about three to five minutes. It has a spoken script and a written version the member can read in email without playing the audio. Kickoff episodes teach the Track Skill for the member's role and recent work; Practice episodes teach the chosen Practice — explaining a tool when the Practice is one, or using one or two tools already named in the member's context. Prefer "episode" or "written version" in manager-facing copy; do not call the dispatch itself a "digest".
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Surfaces

The pages a manager and a member actually work in. Use these names in any instruction, because they match what is on screen.

| Surface | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Deck** | The person's full Skill profile, with scores and confidence |
| **Overview** | Cycle progress and Track summary entry points |
| **Tracks** | The member's current and past Tracks. Open a Track for **Overview** (stage progress) and **Conversations** (Advisor threads) |
| **Journal** | The member's reading feed of dispatches: Evidence Evaluated, Advisor Pick, Kickoff, Weekly Prep, Weekly Review, and Wrap-Up. Managers read it on the member page. For Track mail, prefer **Tracks** / **Conversations** |
| **Team page** | Two tabs: **Members** (the member list and sub-teams, with Cycle stats and the member Tracks timeline when Tracks are on) and **Team settings** (team Focus Skills, member opt-in, and Track Settings) |
| **Member page** | One person, seen by their manager: Deck, Journal, and Settings, plus Overview and Tracks when Tracks are on. Prep for coaching in the order Overview → Tracks / Conversations → Deck |
| **Company settings** | Where an Owner or Admin sets the company's Focus Skills and whether members can choose their own |
| **Settings → Focus Skills** | Where a person sees their Focus Skills, and chooses them when allowed |
| **Track Settings** | Where Skill Tracks and automation are configured, for a team or a member |
| **Advisor Picks** | Private audio episodes, weekly and per Track, each with a written description the member can read in email |
| **Currency** | Settings → Currency. A person pins USD, BRL, or automatic (location). Catalog prices and checkout follow that pin. A card issued in Brazil is charged in BRL. |

## Organizations and people

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Organization">
    The customer account. Holds the subscription, the members, and the teams.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team">
    A group of people inside an organization, with a manager. Team Focus Skills, Skill Tracks, and automation are set up here.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Organization role">
    Account-level permissions (`OrganizationRole`): **Owner** has full control including ownership transfer; **Admin** manages members, teams, and settings; **Billing** manages billing and subscription only; **Member** uses the product without admin controls.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team role">
    Team-level permissions (`TeamRole`): **Manager** or **Member**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Focus Skills">
    The Skills that matter most for a person, set for a company, a team, or the person, based on job scope, personal challenges, or organizational values. The person's own win, then their teams', then their companies'. They decide which Skills get meeting Feedback, which Skill the weekly Advisor Pick is about, and which Skills a Track can start on. Internally they are the Skill whitelist (`trackable_attributes`). See [The manager role](/product/manager-role#focus-skills).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Management level">
    How often a manager meets their team about development: **Weekly**, **Monthly**, or **Quarterly**. Named for the manager's coaching cadence, not for how often Skill Deck emails the member.

    See the [Manager Coaching Playbook](/playbooks/manager-coaching/overview).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Other terms you will meet

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Insight">
    An observation the product surfaces about a person or a team, derived from their accumulated evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Superpower">
    An insight highlighting a standout strength, shareable as a public page or image.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quota">
    The internal metering that bounds how much processing a plan gets.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Integration">
    A connected external account (`IntegrationPlatform`): Google Calendar, Microsoft Calendar, Zoom, or Slack.

    Each integration has its own job. Calendars schedule the bots; Zoom needs its own connection so a bot can join Zoom meetings; Slack needs the user to pick channels. The evidence itself comes from **meeting bots** and **Slack channels**. Do not ask new customers to connect Slack at the start.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Attendee">
    The third-party service that supplies the meeting bots, and that transcribes the meeting and maps speakers. Internal term — customers should hear "the Skill Deck meeting bot".
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Words we do not use

| Never | Why | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competency, attribute (externally) | The product says Skill | Skill |
| Surveillance, monitoring | Repeating the frame reinforces it, even in a denial | Describe what the product does |
| Grading, ranking, judging | Implies comparison between people and a verdict | Scoring, evaluating, levelling |
| AI-powered, powerful, seamless | Says nothing and reads as filler | Name the mechanism |
| Accuracy (about confidence) | Confidence is about evidence volume, not correctness | Confidence |
