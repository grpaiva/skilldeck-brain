Skill Deck is a per-seat SaaS product sold in seat bundles. A team buys a plan, gets a fixed number of seats, and pays monthly.

<Info>
  These are the live prices. See [Canonical facts](/overview/canonical-facts).
</Info>

## Plans

| Plan | Seats | USD / month | BRL / month | USD per seat | BRL per seat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small Team | 5 | \$39.90 | R\$199.00 | \$7.98 | R\$39.80 |
| Growth | 15 | \$99.90 | R\$499.00 | \$6.66 | R\$33.26 |
| Scale Up | 40 | \$199.90 | R\$999.00 | \$4.99 | R\$24.97 |
| Enterprise | Custom | Contact us | Contact us | — | — |

Headline range: **\$4.99 to \$7.98** or **R\$24.97 to R\$39.80** per user per month, depending on plan.

Seats beyond the bundle are billed per extra seat, monthly:

| Plan | Extra seat USD | Extra seat BRL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small Team | \$9.90 | R\$49.90 |
| Growth | \$8.90 | R\$44.90 |
| Scale Up | \$7.90 | R\$39.90 |

Larger bundles get cheaper per seat, so pricing pulls buyers upward. The steepest step down is between Growth and Scale Up, which makes 15 seats the number to push past in a negotiation.

Enterprise is for larger organizations and has no list price. The customer contacts us, and a founder sets its seats and terms with them.

## Free tier and quotas

Alongside the [one-month trial](/company/icp-and-market), the product bounds what a free account can do. Useful to know before promising anything on a call.

| Free allowance | Limit | Resets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting bots | 4 | Monthly |
| Slack channels | 1 | Never |
| Advisor Picks episodes | 1 | Weekly |
| Development reports | 1 | Monthly |
| Superpower generations | 1 | Never |
| Highlight generations | 4 | Never |

Calendar integration is free; the bound is on how many meetings get a bot. A paid seat — an individual [Skill Deck Pro](#skill-deck-pro) plan or a seat in a paying organization — makes every one of these unlimited.

The Slack-channel quota exists, and still: do not ask new customers to connect Slack at the start.

## Skill Deck Pro

An individual plan exists: **Skill Deck Pro**, at **\$9.90 / month** or **\$99.90 / year**, and **R\$49.90 / month** or **R\$499.00 / year**. One plan, two billing intervals, two currencies. The homepage shows it on a **Personal** pricing tab, next to the free allowances above.

It is **not the commercial focus**. We sell to teams. We do not expect individuals to buy Skill Deck as it stands. A future recruitment feature set may change that — planned, not live. Keep Skill Deck Pro out of the default pitch.

## Billing currency

Catalog prices and checkout present in **USD** or **BRL**, chosen by the interface language: Brazilian Portuguese shows BRL, and every other language shows USD. Anyone can pin USD or BRL in Settings → Currency, and the pin wins over the language. Checkout charges the currency the page shows.

A card issued in Brazil can only be charged in BRL. A visitor in Brazil who is shown USD gets a note on the pricing screens that links to Settings → Currency.

## The trial

The one-month free trial in the [go-to-market plan](/company/icp-and-market) is a sales motion, not a product feature. A team uses the product on its real work and measures results on its own people before paying anything.

It runs on the team's actual meetings, which is the only way the product can show anything worth paying for.

## The target

**10,000 paid seats within the first 18 months**, in the first wave.

That is the number the [fundraise](/company/stage-and-fundraising) is sized against, and the number that determines whether the land-grab hypothesis held.

## Reading the economics

  <Card title="Expansion is structural" icon="trending-up">

  <Card title="Cost scales with meetings" icon="gauge">
    Every seat generates evidence that has to be transcribed and evaluated by models. Unlike most SaaS, our variable cost tracks how much a customer actually uses the product.
  </Card>

That second point is worth understanding before quoting discounts. A heavy user costs materially more to serve than a light one, which is why the product meters usage internally.
