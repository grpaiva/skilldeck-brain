Skill Deck is a per-seat SaaS product sold in seat bundles. A team buys a plan, gets a fixed number of licenses, and pays monthly.

## Plans

| Plan | Licenses | Price / month | Effective price per seat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small Teams | 5 | $74.99 | $15.00 |
| Growth | 15 | $199.00 | $13.27 |
| Scale Up | 40 | $499.00 | $12.48 |
| Enterprise | 100 | $999.00 | $9.99 |

Headline range: **$9.90 to $15.00 per user per month**, depending on plan.

<Note>
  The published range starts at $9.90, but the Enterprise plan works out to $9.99 per seat. This is a known inconsistency in the source material, not a second price point. Quote the range as written until a founder settles it, and do not derive a $9.90 tier from it.
</Note>

Larger bundles get cheaper per seat, so pricing pulls buyers upward. The gap between Scale Up and Enterprise is the steepest step down, which makes 40 seats the number to push past in a negotiation.

## The trial

One month, free, for teams that fit the [ICP](/company/icp-and-market). A team uses the product on its real work and measures results on its own people before paying anything.

The trial is not a demo environment. It runs on the team's actual meetings, which is the only way the product can show anything worth paying for.

## The target

**10,000 paid seats within the first 18 months**, in the first wave.

That is the number the [fundraise](/company/stage-and-fundraising) is sized against, and the number that determines whether the land-grab hypothesis held.

## Reading the economics

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Expansion is structural" icon="trending-up">
    Skill Deck is bought per seat by teams that grow. Expansion comes from adding people and from spreading to adjacent teams, not from upselling features.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cost scales with meetings" icon="gauge">
    Every seat generates evidence that has to be transcribed and evaluated by models. Unlike most SaaS, our variable cost tracks how much a customer actually uses the product.
  </Card>
</Columns>

That second point is worth understanding before quoting discounts. A heavy user costs materially more to serve than a light one, which is why the product meters usage internally.

## What we do not have yet

Being straight about this internally is more useful than guessing:

- No published annual pricing or annual discount.
- No self-serve checkout path documented in this brain.
- No formal discount policy. Discounts are founder decisions, case by case.
