## Where we are

Skill Deck is **live and running in version 2.0**, built entirely by the two founders. Two people, no engineering hires, product in production.

We operate with a lean startup mindset: build fast, test, validate, and keep the burn small enough that being wrong is survivable.

<Info>
  Stage language: we are **selling to our first paying customers**. We raise after those customers show us who buys and why.
</Info>

## The goal now: first paying customers

We are investing in outbound and inbound to win our first paying customers. See the [go-to-market motion](/company/icp-and-market). Each sale does two jobs: it brings in revenue, and it teaches us how Skill Deck sells.

From the first customers, we want to learn:

- The strongest sales arguments
- The biggest objections
- The kinds of teams it sells best to
- The markets that show the most interest

## Milestones

<Steps>
  <Step title="Now">
    Version 2.0 live, built by the two founders, no outside capital. Selling to the first paying customers through outbound and inbound.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Next">
    Learn from those customers and improve the sales process with what they teach us.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then">
    Once we understand the sales process and who pays, start looking for an investment round.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How to talk about the stage

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Say this">
    - "We are selling to our first paying customers."
    - "Two founders, product live in 2.0, built entirely by us, no outside capital."
    - "We will raise once our first customers show us who buys and why."
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Not this">
    - Anything implying we are raising now, or that a round is committed.
    - Revenue, ARR, customer counts, or logos. Those numbers are not in this brain and must not be invented.
    - Valuation, terms, or instrument. Founders only.
    - Naming investors, prospective investors, or intro sources.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
